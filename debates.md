# Interpretive debates

[README](README.md) · [Overview](summary/L0-overview.md) · [Parts](summary/L1-parts.md) · [Glossary](glossary.md) · [Source evaluation](sources/EVALUATION.md)

This file sets out the main disputes about how to read *Intention*, neutrally. For each dispute it gives:

- **The text:** what Anscombe says and where, kept to what is uncontroversial.
- **Positions:** who holds what, with a link to the source file the attribution comes from.
- **Fault line:** what the disagreement turns on.

Positions marked *(reported)* come from a source the repo could not read in full. They are known only through the commentator named. See [UNAVAILABLE](sources/UNAVAILABLE.md).

*Editorial note:* the grouping of positions into camps is this repo's own simplification. Most commentators hold nuanced combinations.

---

## 1. Knowledge without observation: what is known, and how central is it?

**The text.**
- **§§6–8.** "Why?" is refused application when the agent did not know they were doing the thing (§6), or knew it only by observation. Anscombe introduces knowledge "without observation": the knowledge one has of the position of one's limbs, and of the cause of a start or jerk ([§08](summary/L3-sections/§08.md)). Intentional actions are a sub-class of the things known without observation.
- **§28.** She returns to the theme: I can say what I am doing without looking.
- **§29.** Doesn't this require two objects, my intention and what actually happens?
- **§§30–31.** She rejects pushing the "real" action back into the agent, and tries and finds wanting an analogy with orders.
- **§32.** The shopping list leads her to ask whether there is "practical knowledge" in the ancient and medieval sense, which she answers in §§45–48.

**Positions.**
- **(a) The agent knows what actually happens: the strong, factive reading.** The agent knows what is happening in the world, not something inner. Held by [Özaltun](sources/secondary/ozaltun-2016-practical-knowledge-section-45.md), [Frey](sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) and [Campbell 2022](sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md) (knowledge "in intention"). [Setiya 2016](sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md) holds it and extends it to completed (perfective) and future facts.
- **(b) The agent knows only the action in progress: the imperfective reading.** What is known is the on-going action ("I am doing X"), not its completion. Held by Thompson *(reported)* and Haddock *(reported, via Özaltun and Hubbs)*, and adopted by [Hubbs](sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md). Özaltun classes this as "modest".
- **(c) The agent knows the description under which the act is intentional.** Moran 2004 *(reported, via [Özaltun](sources/secondary/ozaltun-2016-practical-knowledge-section-45.md))*.
- **(d) The agent knows their intention or trying, not the action.** Grice, Paul and O'Shaughnessy *(reported, via Özaltun and Setiya)*. [Richter's IEP entry](sources/secondary/richter-nd-iep-anscombe.md) glosses practical knowledge as knowledge of one's intentions.
- **On whether §8 is the definition:**
  - [Speaks](sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) reads §§6–8 as a proposed necessary-and-sufficient condition that fails to the counterexamples.
  - [Small](sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) reads non-observationality as only a negative "outline", with §48 as the positive core.
  - [Frey](sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) and [Campbell](sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md) note that non-observation cannot be the distinguishing mark, since limb position and mental causes are also known that way.
  - [Holton](sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) and [Wilson & Shpall](sources/secondary/wilson-shpall-2012-sep-action.md) doubt the criterion itself, on the grounds that proprioception is a kind of perception (the Ian Waterman case).
- **Does intentional action require knowing what one does?**
  - Yes: Thompson, Haddock and Özaltun (as summarised in the [Schwenkler](sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) and [FHS](sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md) files).
  - No, or only qualified: [Setiya 2008](sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md), Davidson's carbon-copier *(reported)*, Schwenkler's guide, ch. 6 (per the [Schwenkler file](sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md)), and McDowell, who thinks practical knowledge needs help from perception *(reported)*.

**Fault line.** Is "knowledge without observation" a criterion (epistemic and negative) or a symptom of a distinct form of knowledge (practical, §48)? And is its object the world or the agent's mind?

## 2. Practical knowledge as "the cause of what it understands" (§48), and failure (§45)

**The text.**
- **§§32 and 45.** In §32, the discrepancy between the shopper's list and what is bought is a mistake in the performance, not in the judgement; Anscombe connects this with a remark of Theophrastus. In §45 the man directing a building project without seeing it knows what is being done, and Anscombe then raises the problem of how that is knowledge if his orders are not carried out.
- **§48.** Anscombe adopts the ancient and medieval formula that practical knowledge is "the cause of what it understands". She says that failure to execute an intention is necessarily the rare exception under the descriptions closest to what one does.

**Positions.**
- **Is it factive?**
  - Yes; Anscombe never says one knows what is false. The Theophrastus remark only locates the mistake ([Holton](sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md); [Özaltun](sources/secondary/ozaltun-2016-practical-knowledge-section-45.md), who traces the remark through §2, §32 and §45).
  - §45 is a "mis-step" by Anscombe ([Small](sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md)).
  - [Sandis](sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md) confines §45's claim that the knowledge is independent of what happens to practical reasoning proper.
  - [Setiya SEP](sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md) calls Anscombe's position here "elusive".
- **What kind of cause?**
  - Always the cause of what it knows: Moran, Stoutland, McDowell and Rödl *(reported by [Setiya 2016](sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md))*; also [Frey](sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) and [Hubbs](sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md).
  - A formal cause, and only where the description is formally that of an executed intention ([Setiya 2016](sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md); [Setiya SEP](sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md)).
  - A formal cause internal to the action ([Campbell 2022](sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md); [Small](sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md); [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum](sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md), who leave open whether it is also efficient).
  - Efficient and reliabilist: Velleman 2007 *(reported by Setiya, who rejects it)*.
  - Thomistic, both formal and efficient: [Schwenkler 2019](sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) (per abstracts).
- **Where the formula comes from.** Schwenkler cites Aquinas, *Summa* I q.14 ([interview](sources/secondary/schwenkler-c2020-316-interview.md)). Small cites *Summa* I-II q.3 a.5 obj.1 ([file](sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md)). These are compatible.
- **Can practical knowledge come through perception?** Schwenkler's guide entertains this (§6.5 of his guide). He later says it is mistaken ([interview](sources/secondary/schwenkler-c2020-316-interview.md)).

**Fault line.** How to hold together "knowledge" (which seems to need truth) and "practical" (the object depends on the knowing) when execution can fail.

## 3. Anscombe and Davidson; causal theories of action

**The text.**
- **§§9–16.** Anscombe distinguishes reasons from "mental causes" (§§9–11) and says a motive is not a cause at all (§12).
- **§19.** She denies that calling an action intentional mentions any extra feature present at the time it is done.
- **§§23–26.** One action can have many descriptions, under some of which it is intentional (§§6, 23–26).
- She does not discuss Davidson, whose "Actions, Reasons, and Causes" appeared in 1963, the year of the 2nd edition.

**Positions.**
- **Co-founders of one standard picture**, sharing the idea of one action under several descriptions, with Davidson adding causation: [Schlosser](sources/secondary/schlosser-2019-sep-agency.md); [Wilson & Shpall](sources/secondary/wilson-shpall-2012-sep-action.md), who speak of the "Davidson/Anscombe thesis".
- **Opposed poles**, an anti-causalist Anscombe against causalist Davidson: [Wiland & Driver](sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md); [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum](sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md); [Setiya SEP](sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md). [Frey](sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) argues that Davidson takes the action-descriptions for granted. [Campbell 2024](sources/secondary/campbell-2024-practical-knowledge-structural-challenge.md) raises deviant causal chains.
- **Not anti-causalist in the usual sense:**
  - [Schwenkler 2019](sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) argues against reading her as an anti-causalist.
  - [Small](sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) warns against the label.
  - [Speaks](sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) holds that §§9–11 deny only that reasons are *mental* causes.
  - Hornsby relocates the dispute to agent causation as "affecting" *(reported in the [FHS file](sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md))*.
  - [Millgram](sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) holds that on her view, whether mental states implement the intention ceases to be a question of philosophical interest.
- **The historical shift.** The FHS volume documents the move from a "Davidsonian" reception of the book to a "new Anscombean" one ([file](sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md)).

**Fault line.** Is the "Why?" question an account of what intentional action *is*, which would compete with causal theories? Or is it an account of the form of a certain kind of explanation, compatible with causal stories at another level?

## 4. Naive action theory and the order of "Why?"

**The text.**
- **§§23–26.** The pump example generates a series of descriptions (moving arm → operating pump → replenishing water supply → poisoning inhabitants). Each later one gives the reason for the earlier (the A–D order, §26).
- **§42.** Setting out practical reasoning in full describes the same order, not a mental process.

**Positions.**
- **Thompson's "naive action theory"** *(reported, via [Thompson REP](sources/secondary/thompson-1998-rep-anscombe.md), [Setiya](sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md), [Hubbs](sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md))*. Reasons for action are given by other actions in progress ("I'm doing A because I'm doing B"). The "Why?"-order is the reverse of end-to-means reasoning. Adopted by [Frey](sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) and [Hubbs](sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md).
- **"How?" runs the A–D order in reverse** ([Small](sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md)). [Wiland & Driver](sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md) call the order of intention and end-to-means reasoning "equivalent". Thompson calls the one the "reverse" of the other. These are compatible: the same order, read in opposite directions.
- **Treated as one special case** ([Millgram](sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md)).
- **Rejected as too restrictive**, because it cannot explain foreknowledge of completed actions ([Setiya 2008](sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md), [2016](sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md)).

**Fault line.** Are intentions for the future and "mental states" explanatorily posterior to action in progress, or prior to it?

## 5. Intention for the future and the unity of the three heads

**The text.**
- **§1.** The topic is introduced under three heads: expression of intention for the future, intentional action, and intention in acting.
- **§§2–4.** Begin with expressions of intention for the future and quickly turn to intentional action (§4).
- **§§50–52.** Return to the future case. What has been said about intention in present action applies to future intention (§50). A prediction is an expression of intention when "Why?" applies to it (§50). Cases where "I am not going to" (intention) and "I am going to" (belief) might be said together (§52).

**Positions.**
- **Unified by a general sense of "expression of intention"**, the genus of answers to "Why?" ([Moran & Stone](sources/secondary/moran-stone-2011-expression-of-intention.md): "immediate elucidation" of the divisions of intention as "inflections of a single form", rather than "connective" strategies; a general and a special use of "expression of intention"). Clark replies that this fails for intentions never acted on ([FHS file](sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md)).
- **Unified by intention in action / intentional action, with future intention derivative:** [Thompson REP](sources/secondary/thompson-1998-rep-anscombe.md), [Setiya SEP](sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md), Capps *(reported in the Schwenkler/FHS files)*.
- **Not a question Anscombe asks.** The three heads are three contexts of one concept, investigated grammatically ([Wiseman](sources/secondary/wiseman-2016-guidebook.md), [2025](sources/secondary/wiseman-2025-philosophy-talk.md)). McGinn calls the univocity question un-Wittgensteinian *(via the Wiseman file)*.
- **§§2 and 50–52 frame the whole book**, with an analogy to Moore's paradox and a link to determinism and freedom ([Millgram](sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md)). [Schwenkler 2019](sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) also links §§50–52 to Wittgenstein on Moore's paradox.
- **Practical foreknowledge is real.** At §52, someone who knows I won't go for a walk knows "the very same thing" I said would happen ([Setiya 2016](sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md); [Holton](sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) and [Small](sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) on the §52 cases).
- **The command analogy in §2** ([Hubbs](sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md)). §2's "mistake in performance" parallels §45 ([Özaltun](sources/secondary/ozaltun-2016-practical-knowledge-section-45.md)).

## 6. Cognitivism: is intending a kind of believing?

**The text.** In §§2–3, expressions of intention are distinguished from estimates of the future by the kind of justification, if any, that is given. In §32 the shopper's list and the detective's record differ in what a discrepancy shows. In §52, "I am going to" can express a belief while "I am not going to" expresses an intention.

**Positions.**
- **Intention involves belief** ([Setiya 2008/09](sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md)). Setiya concedes that Anscombe herself contrasts intention with belief.
- **Intention is a kind of belief:** Marušić & Schwenkler 2018 *(title and thesis only)*.
- **Against:** Davidson, Bratman, Mele and Paul *(reported, via Setiya)*; [Campbell 2024](sources/secondary/campbell-2024-practical-knowledge-structural-challenge.md), who is non-propositionalist.
- **The §32 "direction of fit" gloss**, in which the list is like a desire and the detective's record like a belief: [Wikipedia](sources/secondary/wikipedia-2026-anscombe-intention-section.md) and SEP "Practical Reason" endorse it. [Wiland & Driver](sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md) reject it as Searle's later gloss; for them §32 contrasts two ways of knowing.

## 7. Intention versus foresight (double effect)

**The text.** In §25, a man who knows the water is poisoned but says he didn't care, he was only doing his job of pumping, gives an answer outside the range that defines intentional action. Anscombe asks whether one can settle one's intentions just by what one says they are.

**Positions.**
- [Wiseman](sources/secondary/wiseman-2025-philosophy-talk.md) and [Wiland & Driver](sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md) connect the book's means–end analysis to Anscombe's case against Truman and to double effect.
- [Schwenkler](sources/secondary/schwenkler-c2020-316-interview.md) now holds that his guide's use of the "Why?" test to mark intention off from foresight was "flatly wrong", since foreseen side-effects can pass it.
- Michon offers broad and narrow scopes of intention. Narboux argues that ethics is needed to complete the account ([Haddock & Wiseman file](sources/secondary/haddock-wiseman-2022-anscombean-mind.md)).

## 8. Practical reasoning: calculation, the good, and form (§§33–43)

**The text.**
- **§33.** The practical syllogism is not a demonstration of what one ought to do; it is a different form of reasoning, misunderstood in modern times. A 1963 addition distinguishes "idle" from proper practical syllogisms ([Sandis](sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md)).
- **§§34–35.** The first premise mentions something wanted, but "I want" does not belong among the premises.
- **§§37–39.** Wanting requires a desirability characterisation, beyond which "What for?" lapses (§38). No desirability characterisation compels (§39, *bonum est multiplex*).
- **§§40–42.** "Good" stands to wanting as "true" stands to judging (§40). The mark of practical reasoning is that the thing wanted is at a distance from the action (§41). Setting it out describes an order (§42).

**Positions.**
- **Instrumentalist / calculative reading:** Müller and Vogler *(reported by [Elliott](sources/secondary/elliott-2016-anscombe-practical-truth.md) and [Millgram & Bowman](sources/secondary/millgram-bowman-2026-sep-practical-reason-structure-of-actions.md))*. Müller argues that backward-looking motives (§§13–14) should also count as practical reasoning. Piedrahita replies that *Intention* never claimed to cover all practical rationality ([FHS file](sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md)).
- **"Guise of the good" reading** ([Frey](sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md); Schwenkler ch. 5.4 per the [abstracts](sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md)).
- **Kant.** Rödl reconciles Anscombe with Kant; Newton calls her "pre-critical"; Small holds that she was wrong, in her later paper "Practical Inference", to treat the formal difference of §33 as unimportant ([Haddock & Wiseman file](sources/secondary/haddock-wiseman-2022-anscombean-mind.md)).
- **Target.** Hare's imperative logic is the foil ([Schwenkler interview](sources/secondary/schwenkler-c2020-316-interview.md)).

## 9. What is the book *for*?

- **Ethically and politically driven:** the 1956 Truman protest, double effect, the critique of Hare ([Wiseman 2016/2025](sources/secondary/wiseman-2025-philosophy-talk.md); [Schwenkler](sources/secondary/schwenkler-c2020-316-interview.md); [Wiland & Driver](sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md)).
- **Rooted in pre-1956 philosophy of mind** (the 1948 Somerville report) ([Haddock & Wiseman 2022](sources/secondary/haddock-wiseman-2022-anscombean-mind.md)).
- **The hidden topic is the voluntary** (§49) (Narboux 2022, in [Haddock & Wiseman](sources/secondary/haddock-wiseman-2022-anscombean-mind.md); see also [Narboux 2020](sources/secondary/narboux-2020-anscombes-account-of-voluntary-action.md), a close reading of §49 that argues the book deliberately stops short of ethics). [Schwenkler](sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) instead treats §49 as concluding material.
- **The founding document of analytic action theory** ([Thompson REP](sources/secondary/thompson-1998-rep-anscombe.md)).
