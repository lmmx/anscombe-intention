# Evaluation of secondary accounts

[README](../README.md) · [Unavailable sources](UNAVAILABLE.md) · [Debates](../debates.md)

This file assesses the 35 secondary accounts saved in [`secondary/`](secondary/). For each one it gives the source's accuracy, coverage, interpretive slant, reliability and depth, and says what it can be trusted for.

## How the accounts were checked

- **Benchmark.** Each account's claims about *which section says what* were checked against Anscombe's own analytic table of contents to the 2nd edition (1963; Harvard paperback 2000, pp. v–ix). That table gives one or more sentences per section plus the page on which each section starts, so it is an authoritative check on section boundaries and on page-to-§ mappings. Where a commentator cites pages rather than sections, the pages were converted to §§ using the contents' start pages. (Pagination differs between the 1957/58 and 1963 printings from p. 59 onward; see [Sandis](secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md).)
- **Limits.** The full text of the book was not available in a form that could be legitimately consulted for this repo. The archive.org copies are lending-restricted. Checks are therefore against the contents line, plus cross-checks between independent commentators. Where two careful readers who quote the text agree (e.g. Setiya, Özaltun, Small and Holton on §§28–32 and §45), that agreement is treated as corroboration.
- **What "accuracy" means here.** Accuracy is fidelity in reporting what the text says and where, not whether the interpretation is right. Interpretive disagreement is recorded separately below and in [debates.md](../debates.md).
- **Depth scale.** 1 means a passing mention or compression. 5 means a close reading able to stand in for the text on the sections it covers.

## Ranked overview

Tiers are about what a reader of *Intention* can rely on, not about philosophical merit.

## Where the commentators disagree

These are set out neutrally in [debates.md](../debates.md). In headline form:

1. **What practical knowledge is knowledge of.**
   - What actually happens (Özaltun, Frey, Campbell 2022; Setiya 2016, extended to completed and future facts).
   - Only the action in progress (Thompson, Haddock, Hubbs).
   - Only the description under which the act is intentional (Moran, as Özaltun reads him).
   - One's intention or trying (Grice, Paul, O'Shaughnessy; Richter's IEP gloss).
2. **§45 and failure.** Anscombe never claims knowledge of what is false (Holton, Özaltun). Or §45 is a "mis-step" (Small).
3. **"Cause of what it understands" (§48).**
   - Always the cause (Moran, Stoutland, McDowell and Rödl, as Setiya reports them; also Frey and Hubbs).
   - A formal cause only under descriptions that are formally of executed intentions (Setiya).
   - Formal and internal (Campbell).
   - Efficient and reliabilist (Velleman, as Setiya reports and rejects).
4. **Anscombe and Davidson.** Co-founders of one "standard" picture (Schlosser; Wilson & Shpall). Or opposed (Wiland & Driver; Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum; Setiya; Frey). Or not anti-causalist in the way usually supposed (Schwenkler; Small; Hornsby, who relocates the dispute to agent causation).
5. **Is non-observational knowledge (§8) definitional?**
   - A proposed definition that fails (Speaks).
   - A negative outline, with §48 as the core (Small).
   - Doubtful in itself, given proprioception (Holton; Wilson & Shpall).
   - Not the defining mark (Frey; Campbell).
6. **The unity of the three heads (§1).**
   - The general sense of "expression of intention", with "immediate elucidation" rather than "connective" strategies ([Moran & Stone](secondary/moran-stone-2011-expression-of-intention.md)).
   - Intention in action (Capps; Thompson; Setiya).
   - Not Anscombe's question at all (Wiseman; McGinn).
7. **Foresight and intention (§25).** Schwenkler retracts his guide's means–end test. Wiseman applies the means–end test to Truman.
8. **The status of §49.** The hidden topic of the book (Narboux) or concluding material (Schwenkler).
9. **The §32 shopping list.** Belief vs desire "direction of fit" (Wikipedia; SEP "Practical Reason") or two ways of knowing (Wiland & Driver). Also, where the "mistake in performance" remark comes from: Theophrastus (Sandis; Özaltun), or the *Magna Moralia* (Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum). These may be compatible (a remark of Theophrastus reported there).

## Gaps in the saved material

- Of the core "new Anscombean" texts, only Moran & Stone was read in full (found during the verification pass). Not read: Thompson's *Life and Action* and his 2011 essay, Rödl, Ford, Haddock, Hornsby, Hursthouse and Vogler. Their views enter the repo only as other commentators report them, and are labelled that way. See [UNAVAILABLE.md](UNAVAILABLE.md).
- No free section-by-section commentary exists for §§33–44. Gosling (1969) is the fullest open-access critique of §§35–40. The L3 files for §§34, 36, 43 and 44 rely more on Anscombe's contents line and on the writers' recollection of the text, which is marked "†". Two further leads were found but not read: Kunz, "An Examination of Wanting in Anscombe's *Intention*" (MA thesis, Mississippi 2013; abstract only), and Bond, "On Desiring the Desirable" (1981; paywalled).
- Davidson's own papers were not saved (copyright). His objections appear as Setiya, Campbell and Özaltun report them.
