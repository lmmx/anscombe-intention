# Practical Knowledge and the Structural Challenge — Lucy Campbell

## What this source is
A systematic paper, not an exegesis. Campbell says "Nothing below is offered as an interpretation of her work." It defends Anscombe's idea that practical knowledge is knowledge "in intention" against a structural worry:
- practical knowledge seems to be **propositional** (knowledge *that* I am walking into town);
- intention seems to be **non-propositional** (an attitude toward a doable, "to walk into town").

It is included here because it states the main positions on the nature of intention clearly and lists the literature on cognitivism. It is not a guide to what the text says.

## Account of Intention
- Campbell opens with Anscombe's denial that agents ask "what is this body bringing about? Ah yes! The opening of a window" (2000, p. 51, §29). She notes that practical knowledge is knowledge "in intention" (p. 57).
- Anscombe recognised that this requires rejecting the "incorrigibly contemplative" conception of knowledge (p. 57, §32). Newstead (2006) voices the orthodox objection.
- Campbell warns that critics often read Anscombe as giving practical knowledge a "reverse direction of fit". On Campbell's account, knowledge has no direction of fit, and Anscombe does not use the metaphor (citing Alvarez 2010; Boyle and Lavin 2010; Frost 2014).
- Existentiality: knowledge "in intention" is knowledge of what a person is *actually* doing. Campbell says Anscombe is explicit about this, "see especially 2000, §§28–30".
- Anscombe distinguishes the "practical syllogism proper" from the "idle practical syllogism" (the 1963 addition to §33; compare the Sandis file in this repo).
- Future-directed practical knowledge: although Anscombe mostly discusses present action, she "clearly does acknowledge" practical knowledge of what one will do.

## Distinctive claims / interpretive moves
- **The Structural Challenge.** Three claims sit uneasily together:
  1. practical knowledge is embodied in intending;
  2. intending is non-propositional;
  3. practical knowledge is propositional.
- **Rejected response (a): give up (2).** Campbell surveys non-propositionalists: Baier, Müller, Rumfitt, Clark, Thompson 2008 ch. 8, Hornsby, Madden, Campbell 2019. She holds that the burden is on propositionalists.
- **Rejected response (b): give up (3).** Abstract "knowledge of an action" is not existential. Knowledge of a concrete doing is no substitute for propositional knowledge. On Frost 2019: Campbell agrees there is no single fact or description that is "the" object of practical knowledge, but denies that this makes it non-propositional.
- **Positive view.** Knowing a fact requires being mentally related to it through a successful exercise of relevant concepts (condition "NC2"). Carrying out an intention to φ *constitutes* the fact that one is φ-ing through a **practical exercise of one's concept of φ-ing**. This meets the condition.
  - It extends to the future: intending to buy mushrooms constitutes it as a fact that one will, given minimal conditions.
  - It offers a diagnosis of causal deviance: the causal theory cannot separate a φ-ing that is itself an exercise of the concept from behaviour merely downstream of one.

## Positions on key debates
- **Knowledge without observation:** Assumed as Anscombe's datum. The paper explains its *form*, not its epistemology. Campbell refers to her 2015 and 2018 work for epistemology.
- **§48:** Implicit. One "constitutes" the fact known by executing the intention.
- **Davidson / causalism:** The "bifurcationist" causal theory is criticised via deviant causal chains.
- **Naive action theory:** Thompson's naive explanation ("frying mushrooms because making stroganoff") is noted as the most fundamental form of action explanation in the "Anscombean action-theory most prominently associated with Michael Thompson".
- **Intention for the future:** Covered in §3.2. Prospective practical knowledge is also knowledge in intention.
- **Cognitivism:** Strong cognitivism (intention = belief) is "put to one side". It avoids the challenge but, on Campbell's account, gains no advantage. The literature listed: Grice 1971; Velleman 1989; Langton 2004; Setiya 2008, 2009; Paul 2009; Marušić & Schwenkler 2018; Clark 2020; Schwenkler 2022; Hauthaler 2023.
