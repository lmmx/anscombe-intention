# Anscombe on Practical Truth — Jay R. Elliott

## What this source is
An essay on how *Intention*'s account of practical reasoning (§§33–41) relates to Anscombe's later writings on **practical truth**:
- "Thought and Action in Aristotle", 1965;
- "Practical Truth", 1993/2005;
- "Die Wahrheit 'Thun'", 1998.

Its first section is a clear exposition of the practical-reasoning sections of *Intention* and of the "instrumentalist" reading associated with Anselm Müller and Candace Vogler. That reading includes Vogler's "Anscombe on Practical Inference" (2001) and *Reasonably Vicious* (2002), neither of which is otherwise accessible in this repo.

## Account of Intention
- **Form, not subject matter.** Practical reasoning is a "special sort of reasoning" (p. 58, §33) marked by a "difference in form" (p. 60, §33). A distinctive subject matter does not make reasoning practical, as the "mince pie syllogism" parody shows (p. 58). Its distinctive aim is "leading to action" (p. 60).
- **Instrumentalism.** The starting point must be something wanted: what is described in the starting proposition "must be wanted in order for the reasoning to lead to any action" (p. 66, §35). Reasoning finds means in the agent's power.
  - "The mark of practical reasoning is that the thing wanted is at a distance from the immediate action" (p. 79, §41).
  - The action can be calculated as a way of "getting or doing or securing" the thing wanted. Distance can be spatial (pump → water-supply), temporal (poisoning → good government), or a "wider description" of the act (lying on the bed as resting).
  - "I ought to do this, so I'll do it" is *not* practical reasoning, because no wider description is given.
- **Wanting under the aspect of good.** Wanting requires seeing the thing "under the aspect of some good" (p. 75, §39). "I want a saucer of mud" invites "what for?" (p. 70, §37). Insisting "it merely so happens" is "fair nonsense" (p. 71). The series of questions ends in a "desirability characterisation". The good conceived "must really be one of the many forms of good" (p. 77, §40). Health and pleasure end the enquiry. "Evil be thou my good" is intelligible via, e.g., "intact liberty" (p. 75). *Bonum est multiplex*.
- **No moral approbation.** Moral approbation "is in fact irrelevant to the logical features of practical reasoning". Hence the deliberately non-approved examples, such as "what befits a Nazi" (p. 72, §38).
- **"What a man ought to do" (pp. 74–76, §39).** Anscombe asks whether one can say "At this moment, I lose all interest in what befits a man" (p. 74 n. 1). The fact that *some* desirability characterisation is required does not make any particular one necessary (p. 76). The question belongs to ethics. She leaves open that the man who says "Evil be thou my good" may be "committing errors of thought" (p. 76).

## Distinctive claims / interpretive moves
- **Vogler's reading.** On *Reasonably Vicious* (p. 196), an instrumentalist cannot convict the clever "worldly man" of any error in practical reasoning.
- **Elliott's reading.** Anscombe's later work keeps instrumentalism but adds practical truth: "things being as a desirer wants them to be". This is the truth an agent *makes* by making her act bear the descriptions under which she intends it.
  - Practical falsity is possible even when reasoning is effective by the agent's lights. Elliott's example: "practising yoga" by lying rigidly on the floor without knowing what yoga is.
  - Vicious agents act under the description "doing well", which is false of their acts. So their practical reasoning necessarily fails to attain practical truth.
- **The Aquinas link.** Elliott suggests practical truth may draw on Aquinas's practical intellect as *causa rerum intellectarum* (*ST* Ia IIae q3 a5), the same source as *Intention* §48's formula.
- **Self-assessment.** Elliott says Anscombe gives "frustratingly little direct argument" that agents act under "doing well". He substitutes a weaker claim about habituated vice.

## Positions on key debates
- **Knowledge without observation / §48:** Not discussed directly. The Aquinas formula is linked to practical truth (n. 36).
- **Davidson / causalism:** Not discussed. The foil is "standard" Humean belief–desire instrumentalism.
- **Naive action theory:** Not discussed.
- **Intention for the future:** Not discussed.
- **Practical reasoning / desirability characterisations:** The core. Elliott reads *Intention* as instrumentalist in an "expansive" sense that includes constitutive "wider description" reasoning, and as ethically neutral in its logic.
- **Cognitivism:** Not discussed.
