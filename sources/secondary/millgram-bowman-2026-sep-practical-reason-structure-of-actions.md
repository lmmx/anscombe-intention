# Practical Reason and the Structure of Actions (SEP) — Elijah Millgram & Margaret Bowman

## What this source is
An SEP entry on theories that read the logic of practical reasoning off the structure of actions (Vogler, Thompson, Korsgaard, Velleman, and others). Anscombe's *Intention* is introduced as the "short classic monograph" from which Vogler's and Thompson's views descend, and the entry contains one compact paragraph-pair contrasting Anscombe's own model with those successors. Short on Anscombe but unusually alert to *differences* between Anscombe and the neo-Anscombeans. (The SEP's "Practical Reason" entry by Wallace & Kiesewetter, by contrast, mentions *Intention* only once, in passing; see final report.)

## Account of Intention (faithful paraphrase/condensation)

**Anti-psychologism and the "Why?" question.** Vogler (2002) attempts an anti-psychologistic turn in the theory of practical rationality, taking herself to follow Anscombe (Vogler 2001, "Anscombe on Practical Inference"), who in turn followed Aquinas. For Vogler, intentional actions are "picked out and segmented into their parts" by Anscombe's "Why?"-questions ("Why are you chopping the nuts?" "I'm making a salad"), giving actions a series of steps toward a termination point. Thompson likewise uses Anscombean "Why?"-questions, then attends to grammatical aspect (progressive vs. perfective) in the answers ("naïve" vs. "sophisticated" rationalisations).

**Anscombe's own model, contrasted.** The "centerpiece" of the book is a "famous description" of nested actions: moving an arm up and down is operating a pump, which is conveying water to a house, which is poisoning the occupants, which is effecting what the authors label "regime change". A single core action — the ongoing arm movement — is repeatedly recharacterised by invoking broader contexts; the in-order-to links can be elicited by a series of "Why?" questions. The authors note that Anscombe "made a point of nonmoralistic illustrations".

Divergences from successors:
- Anscombe attends mainly to **a single core activity** placed in broader contexts; Vogler focuses on **sequences** of distinct activities (steps).
- Thompson's actions are always composed of yet smaller actions; Anscombe allows that **below** the core arm movement the activity is, in the authors' word attributed to her, "preintentional": flexing muscles while pumping is not a further nested intentional action, as shown by the agent's inability to say how he does it — which denies "the central condition for an action's being intentional", namely knowing what you are doing.

**Desirability characterisations.** Anscombe argues that the action under its outermost characterisation in this "matryoshka-doll structure" needs a point — a "desirability characterization" — to be intelligible as action at all (pleasure, health, or, in the pump case, an aspiration to a better future that is *not* a further layer in the means–end nesting because the agent lacks a sense of how regime change brings it about). The authors say this investigation, and whether a point differs from a reason, "is left incomplete", citing her remark (attributed to "sec. 13") that she says "Consider this" with a view to saying "let us not consider it here".

**Velleman.** In discussing Velleman's reduction of practical to theoretical reasoning (decisions as self-fulfilling predictions), the authors note that Velleman "has worked his way around" to a view Anscombe "entertains and rejects" in "sec. 2".

**Attributability.** Anscombean accounts construe the attribution of action to an agent via the special way the agent knows what she is doing — "its being your action takes care of itself" — in contrast to Korsgaard's self-constitution picture.

## Distinctive claims / interpretive moves
- Sharply distinguishes Anscombe from the neo-Anscombeans: core-action-plus-redescription (Anscombe) vs. step-sequences (Vogler) vs. actions-all-the-way-down (Thompson).
- Attributes to Anscombe a floor of "preintentional" bodily activity (muscle contractions) not itself intentional — consonant with §§6, 8, 19 material but cited without § number.
- Treats the knowledge condition as "the central condition" of intentionality for Anscombe.
- Reads §§37–38's desirability characterisation as the "point" of the outermost action and says Anscombe left the topic incomplete.
- Uses the label "regime change" for the poisoner's further ends; this is the authors' gloss.
