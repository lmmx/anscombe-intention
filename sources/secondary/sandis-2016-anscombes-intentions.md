# Anscombe's Intentions — Constantine Sandis

## What this source is
A catalogue and assessment of every change between four versions of the text:
- Anscombe's *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society* paper "Intention" (1956–57);
- the 1st edition of *Intention* (1957);
- its 2nd impression (1958);
- the 2nd edition (1963). The HUP 2000 reprint is identical to 1963.

Sandis's verdict: there is no "early and late *Intention*", but the changes on practical reasoning (§33) are substantive enough to matter. It is the most useful single source in this repo for **edition differences and pagination**. Note that most authors cite "1957" while quoting the 1963 text and its page numbers.

## Account of Intention
Sandis does not offer a general account. His interpretive remarks attach to particular changes:

- **The 1957 PAS paper.** It overlaps with §5 and §§10–15 (motive, intention, mental cause). It omits the pages on non-observational knowledge and simply asserts that one can give the cause of a thought the way one states the place of a pain, "not based on observation".
- **§19 (p. 29), 1958 change.** The example in the argument against an interior "I" (intention) changes from "the movement of his hand will result in its grasping the hammer" to "his muscular contractions will result in his grasping the hammer". Sandis's explanation: Anscombe is already committed to one action under several descriptions (§§23–26), so hand-movement and grasping are not cause and effect. Muscle contractions, by contrast, are not identical to bodily movements under any description. He links this to von Wright's action/result distinction.
- **§2 (1963).** Three changes:
  - "it" becomes "the intention", in the point that it is hard to see why intention is essentially connected with the future;
  - in the comparison with the earliest historical traditions concerning heroic figures, the bracketed "(except for the Hebrew ones)" is removed and "most of" is inserted before "the earliest";
  - "command" becomes "imperative" in the definition of a description of future action addressed to the agent. This widens the remark to requests and prescriptions.
- **§6 (1963).** "To justify the proposed account" becomes "To clarify the proposed account". A defence is weakened to a clarification.
- **§17 (1963).** "has no application" becomes "is shewn not to apply". The change is stylistic.
- **§33, the substantive changes.**
  - A 1957 footnote on Aristotle's "apodeictic" denial is removed in 1958.
  - 1958 expands the claim that both example syllogisms "necessitate the conclusion". Sandis notes the odd switch between "professing to accept" an order and "believing" premises.
  - On negative general premises, 1958 adds that they lead "only to not doing certain things".
  - 1963 rewrites the opening of §33. It drops "lost to modern philosophy through misinterpretation" and the "apodeictic" point. It adds the "ordinary reasoning" beggar syllogism.
  - 1963 adds a long new passage: John driving from Chartres to Paris, and **three types of case**: theoretical syllogism, the *idle* practical syllogism ("a classroom example"), and the practical syllogism proper, whose conclusion is "an action whose point is shewn by the premises… on active service".
  - The added passage pushes later pagination off by almost a page from p. 59 onward.
- **§34 (1963).** A categorical opening becomes a rhetorical question.
- **Link to §45.** The idle/proper distinction matters for §45. The man "merely considering speculatively how a thing might be done" (p. 82) is an instance of idle practical reasoning. So the §45 remark "my knowledge is independent of what actually happens" should be restricted to practical knowledge *proper*, combined with Theophrastus' point on performance.

## Distinctive claims / interpretive moves
- Anscombe's changes to §33 coincide with her work on "Thought and Action in Aristotle" (1965). Sandis suggests her rethinking of Aristotle drove them, but allows that the influence could run the other way.
- **Translations.** All existing translations (German, Spanish, French, Italian) are of the 1963 text. All but the German omit Anscombe's note on changes. The Spanish professes to translate 1957.
- **Citation practices.** Davidson cites a "1959" edition. Teichmann cites 1963. Bayne mixes numbering and dates.

## Positions on key debates
- **Knowledge without observation:** Notes it is absent from the 1957 PAS paper, which only asserts it.
- **§48:** Not discussed.
- **Davidson:** Only the bibliographic point, plus Davidson's claim that all actions are intentional under some description ("Agency"). Sandis says Anscombe "would have agreed" with that claim but would not count muscle contractions as redescribable bodily movements.
- **Naive action theory / cognitivism:** Not discussed.
- **Intention for the future:** Records the §2 wording changes on future-directedness and on imperatives.
- **Practical reasoning:** The core of the paper. It documents Anscombe's growing distinction between practical reasoning *in form* and reasoning about action *in content*.
