# Agency (SEP) — Markus Schlosser

## What this source is
A broad SEP survey of agency (standard theory, agent causation, mental agency, sense of agency, etc.). Anscombe appears only as co-founder, with Davidson, of the "standard conception" of action. Included because it is a representative example of how general overviews compress *Intention*, including a contestable claim.

## Account of Intention (faithful paraphrase/condensation)
- **Narrow sense of agency.** Agency in the narrow sense — the performance of intentional actions — is "most commonly associated" with Anscombe (1957) and Davidson (1963). Their views "differ significantly", but they share the doctrine that action is to be explained in terms of the intentionality of intentional action.
- **Standard conception, claim 1.** Intentional action is more fundamental than action. On one version (Anscombe 1957; Davidson 1963), one event can be an action under several descriptions and is an action iff intentional under some description; it may be intentional under some descriptions and not others (switch/light/burglar example).
- **Standard conception, claim 2.** According to "Anscombe and Davidson's early view", the connection between acting intentionally and acting for a reason "is identity". "Following Aristotle", both held that acting for a reason is acting in a way that can be rationalised by the premises of a sound **practical syllogism** — major premise the goal, minor the means.
- **Individuation.** Anscombe is placed with Davidson on the coarse-grained ("minimizing") side.
- **Knowledge of action.** Anscombe (1957) "argued that the knowledge of our actions is direct", i.e. not based on observation or inference; the entry then moves to the empirical "sense of agency" literature.

## Distinctive claims / interpretive moves
- Places Anscombe inside a single "standard conception" with Davidson rather than as his opponent.
- Attributes to Anscombe the view that intentional action = action for a reason = action rationalisable by a sound practical syllogism. This sits uneasily with Anscombe's own text: §17 admits "for no particular reason" as an answer that does *not* refuse application of "Why?", and her TOC for §35 says "not every statement of reason for acting shows practical reasoning". Treat as a simplification.
