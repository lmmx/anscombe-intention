# "Anscombe's Intention and Related Issues" — John Schwenkler (interview with Richard Marshall, 3:16)

## What this source is

A long-form online interview in Richard Marshall's 3:16 series. Schwenkler (then editor of the Brains blog) discusses *Intention*, his 2019 OUP guide, and related work (including a co-authored thesis with Berislav Marušić that intending is believing). It is valuable mainly because Schwenkler **corrects his own guide** on two points.

## Structure (chapter → §§ of Intention)

Not applicable (interview). The only structural information about the guide: he says his view on perception and practical knowledge is worked out in its **penultimate chapter** (= Ch6, §§44–48, per schwenkler-2019-guide.md).

## Account of Intention (faithful paraphrase/condensation, attributed)

- **Why the book is hard.** Anscombe gives little context and few references to contemporaries. Schwenkler reads the early discussion of "motive" as "helpfully read as a response to" Gilbert Ryle (*The Concept of Mind*), and the practical-syllogism discussion as centred on a criticism of R. M. Hare (*The Language of Morals*), whose view he calls explicitly consequentialist — something Anscombe found abhorrent. He links this to the claim in "Modern Moral Philosophy" that the obstacle is a lack of adequate philosophy of psychology (action, intention), not weak ethics.
- **Intention as a form of description (§47).** He cites §47: the term "intention" has reference to a *form* of description of events, contrasted with intention as an "extra property" of intentional actions. We bring in the concept of intention whenever we describe an action in a way that invites the question "Why?".
- **Intention vs foresight.** One way to state Anscombe's distinction: only what is done intentionally falls within "the means–end order" elicited by the relevant "Why?". A second way: practical knowledge is "the cause of what it understands", whereas mere foresight lacks this causal role. He stresses the distinction is **not itself ethical**; it belongs to the philosophy of psychology, to be done after "banishing ethics" — while noting Joshua Knobe's experimental evidence that moral judgement shapes how people describe actions.
- **Ethics.** Anscombe holds that intentionally killing the innocent is always wrong, while foreseen deaths of innocents can sometimes be justified (double effect background).
- **Aquinas.** Her Thomism is largely hidden in *Intention* (Aquinas appears only "in a stray footnote"); Schwenkler sees her account of practical knowledge as drawing heavily on *Summa Theologiae* I, q. 14 (on God's knowledge).
- **Wittgenstein / Aristotle.** Acknowledges the Wittgenstein debt (quoting Anscombe's remark that she had no idea not put in her head by Wittgenstein, which he calls an overstatement) but declines to assess it in detail; notes Aristotelian debts, including in her 1956 paper on future contingents.

## Distinctive claims / interpretive moves

1. **Self-correction on foresight.** He now holds that the guide's account "gets things flatly wrong" for a case like polluting a river as a foreseen side effect of raising profits: "In order to increase profits" *can* appropriately answer "Why?" there, so the simple means–end test for intention needs revision (he says he is working on a better account).
2. **Self-correction on perception.** In early papers he argued agential knowledge could be "both practical *and perceptual*"; he now says "I now think this is mistaken", diagnosing the error as a failure to grasp the distinctive *form* of practical reasoning (bus-timing example: a conclusion reached by deciding, not predicting, is not observational knowledge). This partly retracts the line of the guide's Ch6 §6.5 "Practical knowledge through perception?".
3. **Open puzzle.** Explanations of the form "because she is doing A" seem to require knowing that one is doing A, which sits uneasily with his denial that we always know what we intentionally do; he describes himself as in "aporia" here.
4. **Intending is believing** (with Marušić): intending to A is believing, on practical grounds, that one will A — which he presents as fitting Anscombe's "form of description" view.

## Section pointers (§N — what the source says)

- §§12–14 (motive) — read as a response to Ryle.
- §§22–27 — means–end order elicited by "Why?" marks the intended; the guide's handling of foreseen side effects is wrong.
- §33 ff. — practical syllogism discussion aimed at Hare's consequentialism.
- §§45–48 — practical knowledge "the cause of what it understands"; Thomistic source in ST I q. 14.
- §47 — "intention" refers to a *form* of description of events, not an extra property.
