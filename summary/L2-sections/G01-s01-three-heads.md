# G01 — The three heads (§1)

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part I: Preliminaries](../L1-parts.md#part-i-preliminaries) › **G01** · [G02 →](G02-s02-04-intention-for-the-future.md)

## Where this group sits
The opening of the book. Nothing precedes it except the Introduction's note on the editions; §1 sets the agenda for the whole of *Intention* by identifying the three ordinary contexts in which the concept of intention is used and by refusing two shortcuts — the claim that intention is essentially future-directed and the claim that "intention" is simply ambiguous.

## The argument, step by step
1. **Three heads (§1).** We speak of expressions of intention for the future ("I am going to …"), of intentional actions, and of the intention with which something is done.
2. **Against futurity (§1).** "Intention always concerns the future" is false: it fails for intentional action.
3. **Against equivocation (§1).** It is implausible that the word is equivocal across these uses; reaching for "different senses" signals that we are in the dark about the concept.
4. **Method (§1).** No general theory first: proceed piecemeal, beginning with the expression of intention for the future (→ §§2–4).

## Key examples
- The first-person "I am going to …" as the canonical expression of intention for the future (§1).

## What it establishes / hands on
- The *unity problem*: one concept, three uses, whose relations must be exhibited. The answer emerges gradually: §23 shows the three as interchangeable "to a certain extent" in answers to "Why?"; §§50–52 show that intention for the future needs no separate account once intentional action and intention with which are understood; §47 states that "intentional" refers to a form of description of events.
- The *order of inquiry*: future intention (§§2–4) → intentional action (§§5–21) → intention with which (§22 ff.) → back to future intention (§§50–52).

## Commentators on this stretch
- Unity as the central task: [Setiya 2022](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md) (Anscombe unifies without positing a mental state); [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) (unity is the conclusion and holds only "to a certain extent").
- Which head is basic is disputed: intentional action first ([Thompson 1998](../../sources/secondary/thompson-1998-rep-anscombe.md); [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) "action first"); intention in action (Capps in [Teichmann (ed.) 2022](../../sources/secondary/teichmann-2022-oxford-handbook-anscombe.md)); expression of intention in a general sense (Moran & Stone in [Ford, Hornsby & Stoutland 2011](../../sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md)).
- Method: [Wiseman 2016](../../sources/secondary/wiseman-2016-guidebook.md) and [Wiseman 2025](../../sources/secondary/wiseman-2025-philosophy-talk.md) read the heads as three contexts of one concept within a "grammatical investigation", not three things to be connected by theory; McGinn (reported there) finds the univocity question un-Wittgensteinian; [Millgram 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) reads the heads as an order of investigation.
- [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md): the lesson is that any account must explain the heads' close relations.
