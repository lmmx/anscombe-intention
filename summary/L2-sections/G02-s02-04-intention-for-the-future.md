# G02 — Expressions of intention for the future (§§2–4)

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part I: Preliminaries](../L1-parts.md#part-i-preliminaries) › **G02** · [← G01](G01-s01-three-heads.md) · [G03 →](G03-s05-08-why-question-and-its-refusal.md)

## Where this group sits
§1 resolved to begin with expressions of intention for the future. §§2–4 attempt that beginning and show why it cannot be completed on its own terms: the decisive contrast between expressing intention and predicting turns on "reasons for acting", a notion intelligible only through intentional action. The group therefore ends by handing the inquiry over to intentional action (§5).

## The argument, step by step
1. **No intuitive foundation (§2).** We can usually tell a prediction from an expression of intention, but this intuitive grasp cannot ground an account; the same sentence form serves both.
2. **Wide "prediction" (§2).** Anscombe stipulates that estimates, commands/imperatives and expressions of intention are all descriptions of the future ("predictions"). Commands give a model of a description whose correctness lies in execution.
3. **Two kinds of falsity (§2).** An expression of intention may be false as a lie, or because the intention was not carried out. In the latter case the fault lies with the facts, not with what was said — the germ of the Theophrastus point later made explicit (§§32, 45).
4. **Method defended (§3).** Study the verbal expression; introspection of an inner state explains nothing.
5. **Criterion by justification (§3).** Estimates are justified by evidence; expressions of intention, if at all, by reasons for acting (why it would be good or useful for the thing to come about). The reasons/evidence contrast is postponed: it needs the account of intentional action.
6. **Third person (§4).** We can often say with fair certainty what someone intends simply by saying what he is doing.
7. **Against the interior picture (§4).** The sense that an agent is sole authority on his intentions trades on a picture of intention as purely inner; what a man actually does is the first thing to consider — though Anscombe grants that an intention can be complete and purely interior. This motivates the turn to intentional action (§5).

## Key examples
- †"I am going to be sick" (prediction) vs. "I am going to take a walk" (intention) (§2); Speaks reports "I am going to fail this exam" as her prediction example.
- Commands as descriptions of future action meant to get them done (§2).
- Someone sitting in a chair writing, whose intentional activity any observer can describe (§4).

## What it establishes / hands on
- **Reasons vs. evidence** as the mark of expressions of intention (§3), to be explained via the "Why?" question (§§5, 9) and finally applied in §50 (a prediction is an expression of intention when "Why?" applies).
- **Facts at fault, not words** (§2) → orders and the shopping list (§§31–32) → "the mistake is in the performance" (§45).
- **Action first, without behaviourism** (§4) → Part II.
- **Commands as a model** (§2) → §20, §§31–32, §45.

## Commentators on this stretch
- [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md): reads §§2–4 as an implicit priority argument (intention-to-act must be understood via intentional action), and charges §4 with confusing epistemic and metaphysical priority.
- [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md), [Özaltun 2016](../../sources/secondary/ozaltun-2016-practical-knowledge-section-45.md), [Hubbs 2016](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md): §2 as the origin of the Theophrastus point; Holton reads it as locating blame, Özaltun as marking another source of mistake, Hubbs as grounding the command–intention parallel.
- [Setiya 2016](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md): §2 supports practical *foreknowledge*; [Millgram 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md): §§2 and 50–52 frame the book; Velleman's prediction view is one Anscombe dismisses.
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md): Ch. 1 — expressions of intention set the standard of correctness; "action first" but not behaviourist; factualist reading of expressions of intention.
- [Ford, Hornsby & Stoutland 2011](../../sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md): Moran & Stone on expression of intention (special vs. general use); [Teichmann (ed.) 2022](../../sources/secondary/teichmann-2022-oxford-handbook-anscombe.md): Roessler on errors of performance vs. of practical judgement.
- Edition variants in §2 (1963): see [Sandis 2015](../../sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md).
