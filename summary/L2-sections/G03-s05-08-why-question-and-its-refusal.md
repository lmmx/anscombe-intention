# G03 — The question "Why?" and its refusal (§§5–8)

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part II: The Question "Why?"](../L1-parts.md#part-ii-the-question-why) › **G03** · [← G02](G02-s02-04-intention-for-the-future.md) · [G04 →](G04-s09-11-mental-causality.md)

## Where this group sits
Part I ended by turning from intention for the future to intentional action (§4). Part II opens here with the book's central definition and immediately confronts its circularity. Rather than defining "reason for acting", Anscombe fixes the relevant sense of "Why?" negatively, by the answers that refuse it application — and in doing so introduces the notion that will dominate the rest of the book: knowledge without observation.

## The argument, step by step
1. **Definition (§5).** Intentional actions are those to which a certain sense of "Why?" applies — the sense whose positive answer gives a reason for acting.
2. **Circularity (§5).** That sense cannot be explained as "the request for a reason for acting" without presupposing the notion of action; and a "Why?" about a doing can be answered with a cause ("because I was startled"). So proceed negatively.
3. **First refusal: ignorance (§6).** "I was not aware I was doing that" shows the question inapplicable. Since one action has many descriptions, the agent may know it under some and not others — intentionality is relative to description.
4. **Second refusal, shelved: involuntariness (§7).** "It was involuntary" would refuse the question, but appealing to it begs the question, and the concept is itself unclear.
5. **Knowledge without observation (§8).** One knows e.g. the position of one's limbs without observation. Movements known only by observation (the gut) are already excluded. Some movements are known without observation together with their cause (a start at something seen); one class of involuntary movements is defined without circularity as those known without observation but with no room for non-observational knowledge of the cause (the sleep-jerk). Intentional actions are a sub-class of things known without observation.

## Key examples
- "I knocked the cup off the table because I was startled" (§5).
- A start or gasp vs. sending for a taxi (§5, per Small).
- Sawing a plank / Smith's plank / a squeaky noise (§6).
- Limb positions; peristalsis; the jerk on falling asleep; jumping at a crocodile (§8).

## What it establishes / hands on
- The **"Why?" criterion** and its description-relativity — basis for §§16–18, the A–D order (§§23–26), and §47.
- **Knowledge of what one is doing** as a necessary condition (§6) — the "knowledge condition" that §§28–32 and §§45–48 develop into practical knowledge.
- **Knowledge without observation** (§8) — the topic of Part IV.
- An unfinished task: movement and cause known without observation are still not enough (the start at the crocodile), so mental causes must be distinguished from reasons (G04).

## Commentators on this stretch
- Definition vs. outline: [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) reconstructs a biconditional (non-observational knowledge that and why) and judges it refuted by counterexamples; [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) reads §§5–8 as a negative "outline" whose core is §48's practical knowledge; [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) extracts three epistemic conditions C1–C3 but later denies knowledge is strictly necessary; [Thompson 1998](../../sources/secondary/thompson-1998-rep-anscombe.md) reads a sequence of filtering tests.
- Necessity of knowledge: [Setiya 2008](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md) ("bold assumption", weakened by the carbon-copier) vs. [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md) (knowledge condition).
- The §8 criterion of "without observation": criticised by [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) and [Wilson & Shpall 2012](../../sources/secondary/wilson-shpall-2012-sep-action.md) (proprioception as perception); [Frey 2016](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) and [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md) deny it is the defining mark of practical knowledge.
- Mis-citations to note: [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum 2023](../../sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md) cite §§9–10 for §8's limb-position material and §42 for §6's refusal; [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) cites §7 for the sleep-jerk (§8).
