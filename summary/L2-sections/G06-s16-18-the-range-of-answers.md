# G06 — The range of answers (§§16–18)

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part II: The Question "Why?"](../L1-parts.md#part-ii-the-question-why) › **G06** · [← G05](G05-s12-15-motives.md) · [G07 →](G07-s19-21-no-extra-feature-and-chains-of-reasons.md)

## Where this group sits
After the negative work of §§5–11 and the positive classification of motives in §§12–15, Anscombe consolidates. §16 summarises; §§17–18 handle the limiting answers ("for no particular reason", "I don't know why I did it") and close Part II by identifying the special "Why?" through the *range* of answers it expects — the range that defines intentional action.

## The argument, step by step
1. **Summary (§16).** "Why?" is refused when the agent did not know he was doing the thing, knew it only by observation, or can answer only with evidence or a (mental) cause. Where it applies, the answer may cite past history taken as good or harm, give an interpretation, or mention something future.
2. **Nil answers accept the question (§17).** "For no particular reason" is like answering "None" to how much money is in one's pocket, not like having no pocket; so intentional action can be for no reason. "I don't know why I did it" likewise does not refuse the question, though Anscombe calls it "a curious intermediary case" in which "Why?" both has and has not application (see [§17](../L3-sections/§17.md) Notes).
3. **But not always intelligibly (§18).** That "no particular reason" is sometimes allowable does not mean it always makes sense; where it does not, the failure is that we cannot understand the man, not that a form of words is barred by language.
4. **Identification (§18).** The special "Why?" is the one that looks for an answer within this range; the range defines the class of intentional actions — a characterisation by the logic of a question, not by a psychological item.

## Key examples
- "None" in one's pocket vs. having no pockets (§17, per Holton and Small).
- Spreading all the green books in the house on the roof "for no particular reason" (§18; corroborated, see [§18](../L3-sections/§18.md) Notes).

## What it establishes / hands on
- A **complete first characterisation** of intentional action by the range of answers to "Why?" (§18).
- The puzzle for §19: if this is what it is to be intentional, what does calling an action "intentional" add? (Answer: no extra feature present at the time.)
- The limit for §§20–21: "no particular reason" cannot be the *only* kind of answer, or there would be no "Why?" and no intentional action; some chains of reasons must exist.
- Intelligibility as a constraint on reasons, later developed via desirability characterisations (§§37–40) and "I just want to" (§51).

## Commentators on this stretch
- [Wiland & Driver 2022](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md): §16's list is a "hodgepodge", after which Anscombe shifts method; they read the book backwards from §47.
- Intentional action ≠ action for a reason: [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md), [Setiya 2022](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md) (idle action as a problem for unification via intention-with-which); contrast [Schlosser 2019](../../sources/secondary/schlosser-2019-sep-agency.md), whose identification conflicts with §17.
- [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md): extracts three criteria from §16; uses §17 to object to §20.
- [Millgram 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md): end of §18 as evidence of anti-psychologism; "no reason" answers mark limits of the pumping model.
- Divergence on §16: [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) classes mental causes as cases where the question gets application.
- [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md): acts for no particular reason manifest neither practical truth nor falsity.
- §17's 1963 wording change is stylistic ([Sandis 2015](../../sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md)).
