# G07 — No extra feature, and the need for chains of reasons (§§19–21)

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part III: The Unity of Action](../L1-parts.md#part-iii-the-unity-of-action) › **G07** · [← G06](G06-s16-18-the-range-of-answers.md) · [G08 →](G08-s22-27-intention-with-which-and-the-a-d-order.md)

## Where this group sits
Part II (§§5–18) marked off intentional actions as those to which a special sense of "Why?" applies, and §18 identified that question by the range of answers it expects — a range that includes "For no particular reason" (§17). G07 opens Part III by drawing three consequences. Calling an action intentional does not report an extra ingredient (§19). Intentional action is not self-standing but depends on the other two heads of §1 (§20). And although there is no proof of a single final end, there must be chains of reasons (§21).

## The argument, step by step
1. Calling an action intentional mentions no extra feature existing when it is done; suppose there were one, and it would relate only contingently to the description under which the action is intentional (§19; reconstruction in [Speaks](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md)).
2. So "intentional" classifies an action as belonging to the class where "Why?" applies (§§18–19).
3. Could that class exist without expressions of intention for the future or further intentions? "Intentional" is not a perceptible cast of a movement, like sadness in a face (§20, per Speaks).
4. If "For no particular reason" were the only answer ever given, "Why?" would lose its special sense, and intentional action could not be told apart from involuntary movements known without observation (§20).
5. Commandability is considered and found idle as a mark of the intentional; "Tremble!" shows that responding to a command is not the same as obeying it (§20; [Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md), [Hubbs](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md)).
6. The Aristotelian proof that there is one final end for all action is criticised (§21).
7. Even so, some chains of reasons must exist wherever there is intentional action (§21).

## Key examples
- An inner feature supposed to make a movement intentional; the hammer-grasping example, revised in 1958 ([Sandis](../../sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md)) (§19).
- The sad face as a model to be rejected (§20, Speaks).
- "Tremble!" (§20, Holton).

## What it establishes / hands on
- The anti-"extra feature" theme returns in §27 (no determining interior act), §§29–30 (acts of will; initiating movements) and §§47–48 ("intentional" as a *form of description*).
- The interdependence of the three heads underlies the unity claims of §23 (going to / am doing / in order to) and §§50–52.
- Chains of reasons (§21) are examined as intention with which (§22) and ordered as the A–D series (§26).

## Sections
- [§19](../L3-sections/§19.md) — "Intentional" names no extra feature present at the time of acting; proof by supposing one.
- [§20](../L3-sections/§20.md) — Intentional action needs further intention and intention for the future; an all-"no reason" practice would contain none.
- [§21](../L3-sections/§21.md) — Aristotle's proof of a final end fails, but some chains of reasons must occur.

## Commentators on this stretch
- **§19.** [Speaks](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) reads it as a metaphysical thesis whose argument works only against intrinsic, physical features. Ford, in [Ford–Hornsby–Stoutland](../../sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md), reads it via a logic of non-accidental species: no differentia is added. [Millgram](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) reads intention as the internal formal cause or control structure of an action (citing Vogler 2019 on §§19–20). [Sandis](../../sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md) records the textual variant.
- **§§20–21.** [Speaks](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) objects that an absent *concept* does not mean absent intentional actions. [Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) sees prescience about deviant causation. [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) reads §§20–21 as making intentional action depend on the practices of the other two heads. [Millgram](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) draws a parallel with Davidson's anti-sceptical arguments.
- Sources are thin on §21.
