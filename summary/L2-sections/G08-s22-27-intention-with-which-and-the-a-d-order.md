# G08 — Intention with which and the A–D order (§§22–27)

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part III: The Unity of Action](../L1-parts.md#part-iii-the-unity-of-action) › **G08** · [← G07](G07-s19-21-no-extra-feature-and-chains-of-reasons.md) · [G09 →](G09-s28-30-knowledge-of-ones-own-action.md)

## Where this group sits
G07 showed that intentional action presupposes further intentions and chains of reasons (§§20–21). G08 examines the third head of §1, intention with which one acts. It gives a test for it (§22). It then uses the invented case of the man at the pump to show how one action carries many descriptions, ordered as means to ends (§§23–26). It closes by rejecting the idea that an interior act settles what is being done (§27). This is the core of Part III, "The Unity of Action". The difficulties of §§24–25 are also where the intention/foresight distinction is located.

## The argument, step by step
1. When the intention with which one does P mentions a future Q, the agent must treat "But if P, Q won't happen" as incompatible with having that intention. His own working sense of means is enough (§22; eclipse example per [Millgram](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md)).
2. Is there *the* description of an action? The pumper is moving his arm, operating the pump, replenishing the water-supply and poisoning the inhabitants. He is also contracting muscles, clicking out a rhythm, and more (§23).
3. Every true description meeting the "Why?" criteria is a description of an intentional action. "I am going to…", "I am …ing" and "in order to…" are to an extent interchangeable answers (§23).
4. Then are there as many actions and intentions as descriptions (§23)? Difficulties: when does he poison them, and how is arm-moving an act of poisoning (§24)?
5. Suppose he knows of the poison but "didn't care" and only wanted his pay. That answer lies outside the defining range. One cannot fix one's intention just by saying what it is, and the truthfulness of such answers can be controlled. Why do intentions matter apart from deeds (§25)?
6. Answer to §23: one action under the series A–D. Each term is the intention with which the previous one is done, and the last is the intention of the whole (§26). "Why?" runs A→D and "How?" runs D→A ([Small](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md)).
7. The series breaks where the agent does not know, does not care, or cannot take success for granted ([Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md)). Below A lie non-intentional descriptions such as muscle contractions (§§23, 26).
8. Is there ever a place for an interior act of intention that really determines what is going on? In general, no (§27).

## Key examples
- The man at the pump and the poisoned water-supply (§§23–26).
- Going upstairs to fetch a camera; going to London to make an uncle change his will (§23, per Holton).
- The pumper who "didn't care", wanting his pay (§25).
- The eclipse (§22, per Millgram).

## What it establishes / hands on
- The **A–D order**: action as a teleological unity represented by an ordered series of descriptions. This is the object of practical knowledge in Part IV (§§28–32, [Small](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md)). It is also the order that the practical syllogism sets out (§42).
- The pump example returns in §§28–31 (the hole in the pipe), and the "right order" theme in §45.
- Intention vs. knowingly bringing about (§25) bears on double effect and on §§48–49.
- The anti-interior conclusion of §27 sets up the "two objects of knowledge" puzzle of §29.

## Commentators on this stretch
- **The order.** [Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) sets out the break points. [Small](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) and [Frey](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) give the "Why?"/"How?" mirror. [Wiland & Driver](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md) speak of "the order of intention", real among events, and equivalent to end–means reasoning. [Thompson 1998](../../sources/secondary/thompson-1998-rep-anscombe.md) calls it "the reverse of" that reasoning. [Millgram](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) treats the "pumping model" as the template of the whole book. [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum](../../sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md) argue that the progressive favours a process ontology.
- **Unity of the heads.** [Setiya SEP](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md) and [Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) take the §23 interchangeability as the key to the three heads. Holton adds that it holds only "to a certain extent".
- **Individuation.** [Speaks](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) and Hornsby, in [Ford–Hornsby–Stoutland](../../sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md), discuss one action under many descriptions. [Wilson & Shpall](../../sources/secondary/wilson-shpall-2012-sep-action.md) and [Schlosser](../../sources/secondary/schlosser-2019-sep-agency.md) call this the "Davidson/Anscombe thesis".
- **Intention vs. foresight (§§24–27).** [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) groups §§24–25 and §27 under this head. In the [3:16 interview](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-c2020-316-interview.md) he concedes that the "Why?"/means–end formulation fails for side-effects. Michon, in [Teichmann (ed.) 2022](../../sources/secondary/teichmann-2022-oxford-handbook-anscombe.md), finds broad and narrow scopes of intention.
- **Known mis-citation.** Holton cites §24 for the "didn't care" case, which the contents line puts in §25.
