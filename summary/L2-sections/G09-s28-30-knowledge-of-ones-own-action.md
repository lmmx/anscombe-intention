# G09 — Knowledge of one's own action, and false ways out (§§28–30)

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part IV: Knowledge Without Observation](../L1-parts.md#part-iv-knowledge-without-observation) › **G09** · [← G08](G08-s22-27-intention-with-which-and-the-a-d-order.md) · [G10 →](G10-s31-32-orders-and-the-shopping-list.md)

## Where this group sits
Part II made knowledge without observation one mark of intentional action (§§6–8). Part III showed that an intentional action extends, through the A–D order, to descriptions far from the agent's body, such as replenishing a water-supply or poisoning the inhabitants (§§23–26). G09 opens Part IV by bringing these together. How can the agent know without observation what he is doing under such wide descriptions (§28)? Anscombe then rejects two tempting retreats: inward to an act of will (§29), and back to an initiating bodily event (§30).

## The argument, step by step
1. I can say what I am doing without looking to see (§28).
2. The topic of an intention may be known or merely believed (by observation, hearsay, superstition). If I believe correctly that doing ABC brings about Z, then doing Z is intentional and not known by observation (§28, p. 50, per [Small](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) and [Setiya 2016](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md)).
3. The difficulty: what happens at a distance seems knowable only by observation. So must there be two objects, my "doing" or intention known without observation and the actual happening known by observation? Two ways of knowing seem to require two things known (§29).
4. The resulting theories of will are rejected. These are acts of will contingently linked to the world (Speaks: early Wittgenstein), and the "mad" or "false avenue" view that whatever I think I do is what I really do (§29, p. 52).
5. "I do what happens." The agent does not observe his body to learn what it is bringing about. Observation can be an aid, as the eyes are in writing, but one can write with eyes shut (§29).
6. Nor is the real act an initiating physical event. Pushing the object of knowledge "back and back" fails, because we know what we do without knowing how we physically do it (§30; [Setiya 2008](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md), [Speaks](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md)).

## Key examples
- Painting a wall yellow (§§28–29, per [Özaltun](../../sources/secondary/ozaltun-2016-practical-knowledge-section-45.md)).
- The observer-agent who discovers that his body is opening a window (§29, per [Campbell 2024](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2024-practical-knowledge-structural-challenge.md)).
- Writing with eyes shut (§29).
- Keeping an object level with a pumping contraption, and a physiologist's experiment (§30, per Setiya 2008 and Small).

## What it establishes / hands on
- The object of the agent's knowledge is the worldly action under its intentional descriptions. It is neither an inner act nor a first bodily cause.
- The puzzle of one object known in two ways stays open. It is addressed by the order analogy (§31), the shopping list (§32), practical reasoning (§§33–43), the builder (§45) and "cause of what it understands" (§48).
- The contrast between true opinion about means (§28) and "knowing one's way about" (§48) is a tension noted by Small.

## Commentators on this stretch
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md): "Raising difficulties" (§28) and "False avenues of escape" (§§29–30). The three avenues are knowing what I will, knowing how I move, and doing what I think. He links the stretch to Wittgenstein's *Blue Book* and "The First Person".
- [Setiya 2008](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md) and [2016](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md) hold that the key claim is non-inferential knowledge. He uses "I do what happens" against interiorising and progressive-only readings.
- [Small](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md), [Frey](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md), [Özaltun](../../sources/secondary/ozaltun-2016-practical-knowledge-section-45.md) and [Hubbs](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md) agree that the object is the worldly happening. Hubbs, following Thompson, holds that in the good case agent and observer know the same fact.
- Several commentators deny that non-observation is the defining mark of practical knowledge, since limb position and mental causes are also known without observation: [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md), [Frey](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md), and Haddock in [Ford–Hornsby–Stoutland](../../sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md).
- McDowell vs. Özaltun, in [Haddock & Wiseman 2022](../../sources/secondary/haddock-wiseman-2022-anscombean-mind.md), on whether practical knowledge needs receptive aid and whether Anscombe holds that all intentional action is known.
- [Speaks](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) calls the target a "two-factor" picture and notes a brain-state version that Anscombe does not consider. [Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) raises proprioception doubts (Ian Waterman).
