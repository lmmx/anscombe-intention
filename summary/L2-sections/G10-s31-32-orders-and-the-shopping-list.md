# G10 — Orders and the shopping list (§§31–32)

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part IV: Knowledge Without Observation](../L1-parts.md#part-iv-knowledge-without-observation) › **G10** · [← G09](G09-s28-30-knowledge-of-ones-own-action.md) · [G11 →](G11-s33-35-practical-syllogism.md)

## Where this group sits
G09 left Anscombe with a puzzle. The agent knows without observation what he is doing, such as replenishing the water-supply. Yet an observed fact, such as a hole in the pipe, can show his statement false. She has refused to make his knowledge an inner object instead (§§29–30). G10 begins a solution. First she tries an analogy with orders and finds it inadequate (§31). Then the shopping list shows a kind of knowledge whose failures are failures of performance (§32), which leads to the question of "practical knowledge" and on to practical reasoning (§33).

## The argument, step by step
1. Attempted solution: observed facts that defeat "I am replenishing the water-supply" are like facts that make an order fall to the ground. A soldier ordered to clench his teeth hands over his false teeth (§31; [Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md), [Hubbs](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md)).
2. The contradictory of an order is an order. The contradictory of a statement of intentional action is "Oh no you aren't", said by someone setting out to hole the pipe with a pick-axe. It is not a report of the hole (§31).
3. Inadequacy: an order is not a piece of knowledge, but the agent's statement claims to describe what is happening (§31, contents line; Hubbs).
4. The shopper's list and the detective's list may have the same content. When the list and the purchases disagree, the detective's list is at fault, but in the shopper's case the fault is in the performance (§32).
5. Failure to do what one intends is a mistake in performance, not in judgement. It is analogous to failure to execute an accepted order, and unlike disobedience. An example is "Now I press button A" (§32; [Setiya 2016](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md), Hubbs).
6. Modern philosophy's "incorrigibly contemplative conception of knowledge" blocks understanding of the ancient and medieval notion of practical knowledge (§32, p. 57). §33 says it must be approached through practical reasoning.

## Key examples
- The court-martialled soldier with false teeth (*This England*) (§31).
- The hole in the pipe vs. the pick-axe (§31).
- The shopping list and the detective (§32).
- "Now I press button A" (§32).

## What it establishes / hands on
- Statements of intentional action resemble orders in how they fail, but they are claims to knowledge.
- The performance/judgement contrast is first found in §2 and recurs in §45.
- The concept of practical knowledge leads to practical reasoning (§§33–43), to the builder (§45), and to "the cause of what it understands" (§48).

## Commentators on this stretch
- **§31.** [Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) presents it as "The solution" and develops a presupposition-failure, "open knowledge" reading. The contents line, by contrast, calls it attempted and inadequate. [Hubbs](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md) says the parallel "fails at the centre". [Millgram](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) holds that the contradictory of an intention is an intention, and a failed precondition defeats only a means. [Frey](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) counts contradiction by an opposing intention as a mark of practical knowledge.
- **§32: direction of fit.** A "direction of fit" reading of the shopping list is endorsed or used by [Wikipedia](../../sources/secondary/wikipedia-2026-anscombe-intention-section.md) and [Thompson 1998](../../sources/secondary/thompson-1998-rep-anscombe.md), and following Searle by the SEP *Practical Reason* entry (not saved). It is rejected by [Wiland & Driver](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md), who say the passage concerns two ways of knowing, not belief vs. desire. [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md) and [Campbell 2024](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2024-practical-knowledge-structural-challenge.md) also reject it, as does [Millgram](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md), citing Vogler.
- **§32: performance vs. judgement.** [Setiya SEP](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md) calls it a normative contrast and finds the epistemology "elusive". [Setiya 2008](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md) dissents, holding that the mistake is of both performance and judgement. [Özaltun](../../sources/secondary/ozaltun-2016-practical-knowledge-section-45.md) reads it as another source of error, not knowledge independent of the facts. Roessler, in [Teichmann (ed.) 2022](../../sources/secondary/teichmann-2022-oxford-handbook-anscombe.md), adds errors of practical judgement.
- **Attribution of the remark.** The remark that the mistake lies in the performance is called "Theophrastus' point" by [Sandis](../../sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md). [Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) says it is from the *Magna Moralia* 1189b and that Anscombe attributes it to Theophrastus. [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum](../../sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md) say she borrows it from the *Magna Moralia*. The text should be checked.
