# G11 — The practical syllogism (§§33–35)

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part V: Practical Reasoning](../L1-parts.md#part-v-practical-reasoning) › **G11** · [← G10](G10-s31-32-orders-and-the-shopping-list.md) · [G12 →](G12-s36-40-wanting-and-desirability.md)

## Where this group sits
Part IV (§§28–32) ended with the shopping list and a question: is there "practical knowledge" in the ancient and medieval sense (§32)? Anscombe does not answer directly. §33 says such knowledge can be understood only once practical reasoning is understood. That opens Part V, a detour through Aristotle's practical syllogism that lasts until §43. G11 contains the core logical claims: what kind of reasoning the practical syllogism is, how it differs from proof, and what it must start from.

## The argument, step by step
1. Practical knowledge presupposes an understanding of practical reasoning (§33).
2. The practical syllogism is commonly misread as ordinary reasoning to a conclusion of the form "I ought to do X" (the beggar example). A practical subject matter does not make reasoning practical (the "mince pie" parody) (§33).
3. Aristotle's universal premises, taken as premises of action, are "insane". The premises do not necessitate the action as theoretical premises necessitate a conclusion, yet this is no defect (§33; dry food; vitamin X and pigs' tripes).
4. *(1963 text)* Aristotle is partly to blame, for classifying reasoning by subject matter. Theoretical reasoning can concern variable things (John from Chartres to Paris). The real difference is a "difference of form". There are three cases: the theoretical syllogism, the "idle" practical syllogism, and the practical syllogism proper, whose conclusion is an action and whose premises are "on active service" (§33).
5. So "practical reasoning" need not look like a proof syllogism. Its starting point is something wanted, mentioned in the first premise (§34).
6. Aristotle's first premises are evaluative. "I want" is not itself a premise, though what the first premise describes must be wanted. Not every reason for acting displays practical reasoning (§35).

## Key examples
- The beggar syllogism and the "mince pie syllogism" (§33).
- Aristotle's dry-food syllogism; the "insane" premise to eat all dry food (§33).
- Vitamin X and pigs' tripes (§33).
- John driving from Chartres to Paris (§33, 1963 addition).

## What it establishes / hands on
- Practical and theoretical reasoning are distinguished by **form**, i.e. by how the conclusion relates to the premises (an action whose point they show), not by subject matter. Setiya and Schwenkler take this as the basis of Anscombe's anti-cognitivism.
- The starting point must be **something wanted**. This hands G12 its topic: what wanting is (§36) and how it must be characterised (§§37–40).
- The idle/proper distinction is later used to restrict §45's "knowledge independent of what happens" (Sandis).
- §42 explains that the syllogism displays an *order*, not a mental process.

## Sections
- [§33](../L3-sections/§33.md) — Practical knowledge requires practical reasoning. The practical syllogism is not a proof of "ought"; it differs in form; idle vs proper (1963).

## Commentators on this stretch
- **Textual history:** [Sandis 2015](../../sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md). §33 is the main site of revision in 1958 and 1963, and "difference of form" and idle vs proper are 1963 additions.
- **Form, not content; against Hare:** [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) and the [3:16 interview](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-c2020-316-interview.md). [Setiya 2022](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md) contrasts this with Velleman's cognitivism.
- **Instrumentalist reading** (Müller, Vogler) via [Elliott 2016](../../sources/secondary/elliott-2016-anscombe-practical-truth.md). Müller's objection about backward-looking reasons is reported in [Ford, Hornsby & Stoutland 2011](../../sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md).
- **Practical reasoning as order:** [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md), [Campbell 2024](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2024-practical-knowledge-structural-challenge.md), [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md), [Frey 2016](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md).
- **Kantian contrasts:** Rödl reconciles Anscombe with Kant (Ford et al. 2011). Newton calls her "pre-critical", and Small defends "difference of form" against her later "Practical Inference" ([Haddock & Wiseman 2022](../../sources/secondary/haddock-wiseman-2022-anscombean-mind.md)).
- **Caution:** [Schlosser 2019](../../sources/secondary/schlosser-2019-sep-agency.md) over-reads the syllogism as the form of *all* intentional action, contrary to §17 and §35.
