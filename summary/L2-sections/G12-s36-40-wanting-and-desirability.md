# G12 — Wanting and desirability characterisations (§§36–40)

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part V: Practical Reasoning](../L1-parts.md#part-v-practical-reasoning) › **G12** · [← G11](G11-s33-35-practical-syllogism.md) · [G13 →](G13-s41-43-the-order-of-practical-reasoning.md)

## Where this group sits
G11 concluded that practical reasoning starts from something wanted, and that the first premise mentions it in evaluative terms (§§34–35). G12 asks what that wanting is and what it requires. This is the stretch usually associated with Anscombe's version of the "guise of the good" thesis.

## The argument, step by step
1. The relevant sense of wanting is the one whose primitive sign is trying to get. In this sense not every describable thing can be wanted. Volition and sense-knowledge cannot be described independently. Two problems follow: wanting a wife, and wanting what one does not suppose to exist yet (§36).
2. Anyone who wants something can be asked "What for?", or in what respect it is desirable, until a **desirability characterisation** is given. A want for nothing at all, such as the saucer of mud, is unintelligible. "Anything can be desired" is "fair nonsense" (§37).
3. Once a desirability characterisation is reached, "What for?" cannot sensibly go on. The logic of practical reasoning is indifferent to moral approval, as the Nazi example shows. Formal validity still does not shield the reasoning from criticism (§38).
4. That *some* desirability characterisation is required does not make any *particular* one compulsory: *bonum est multiplex*. Whether a particular good, such as what befits a man, binds everyone is a question for ethics. "Evil be thou my good" is intelligible under some aspect of good (§39).
5. Wanting stands to good as judging stands to true. The good under which a thing is wanted must really be one of the forms of good (§40).

## Key examples
- Wanting a wife; wanting what does not yet exist (§36).
- The saucer of mud (§37).
- "It befits a Nazi to spend his last hour exterminating Jews" (§38).
- Health and pleasure; "at this moment I lose all interest in what befits a man"; "Evil be thou my good" (§39).

## What it establishes / hands on
- Wanting is **formally** directed to the good. This is a constraint on intelligibility, not a moral endorsement. Schwenkler calls it a "formal restriction" (§§37–40).
- It explains why answers to "Why?" that cite further intentions eventually end, and how they end: at a desirability characterisation. This links back to §§21–23 and forward to §48, where failure to attain the desirability characterisation is said to be common.
- It hands G13 a question: with wanting secured, what marks reasoning from it as practical (§41)?

## Commentators on this stretch
- **Exposition:** [Elliott 2016](../../sources/secondary/elliott-2016-anscombe-practical-truth.md) gives the fullest saved paraphrase, with page numbers. Note his p. 72 and p. 78 mis-citations.
- **Instrumentalism and vice:** Vogler, as reported by Elliott, holds that an instrumentalist cannot convict the clever vicious agent of error. Elliott replies through Anscombe's later "practical truth". [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md) distinguishes thin and ethical practical truth.
- **Guise of the good:** [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) (5.4, §§37–41). [Frey 2016](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) argues that the knowledge and goodness requirements are inseparable. [Setiya 2022](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md) gives "trying to get" (§36).
- **Structure:** [Millgram 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) holds that desirability characterisations stop one kind of question but not others, and that knowing what one is doing is the desirability characterisation of action as such. [Millgram & Bowman 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-bowman-2026-sep-practical-reason-structure-of-actions.md) note that the topic is left incomplete.
- **Truth of wanting:** [Teichmann 2008](../../sources/secondary/teichmann-2008-philosophy-of-anscombe.md), second-hand.
- **Not Anscombe's examples:** [Richter n.d.](../../sources/secondary/richter-nd-iep-anscombe.md)'s green books on the roof.
- **Early critics:**
  - [Gosling 1969](../../sources/secondary/gosling-1969-desirability-characterisations.md), "Desirability Characterisations". He argues that the "stopping" claim holds only for questions about the form of an attraction, not for practical reasoning generally (breakfast, sybarite, chess cases).
  - [Platts 1985](../../sources/secondary/platts-1985-object-of-desire.md), "The Object of Desire". He links desirability characterisations to Aquinas's "imagined good" and defends a "halfway house" in which something is seen as desirable without an adequate characterisation.
