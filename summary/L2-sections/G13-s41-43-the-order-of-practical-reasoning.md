# G13 — The order of practical reasoning (§§41–43)

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part V: Practical Reasoning](../L1-parts.md#part-v-practical-reasoning) › **G13** · [← G12](G12-s36-40-wanting-and-desirability.md) · [G14 →](G14-s44-45-practical-knowledge-and-failure.md)

## Where this group sits
G11 showed that practical reasoning differs from proof in form and starts from something wanted. G12 showed that what is wanted must be wanted under a desirability characterisation. G13 returns to the *structure* of practical reasoning. It says what marks reasoning as practical, and what the syllogism describes: not a mental process, but the order already found in intentional action in §§23–26. This identification closes Part V and prepares Anscombe's return to practical knowledge (§§44–48).

## The argument, step by step
1. Practical reasoning is marked by distance. The thing wanted lies at a distance from the immediate action, and the action is calculated as a way of getting, doing or securing it. The distance may be spatial, temporal, or a wider description of the same act (§41).
2. Written out in full, practical reasonings look absurd as reports of thinking. That is because they describe an order, not psychological goings-on: an "order which is there" whenever actions are done with intentions (§42).
3. That order is the one found in discussing "the intentional action", i.e. the A–D series of descriptions elicited by "Why?". Practical reasoning runs through it from end to means, the reverse direction (§42; §§23–26).
4. A contrast brings out how much this order carries. "The stove is burning" describes a physical process; "he is paying his gas bill" describes a human doing of enormous apparent complexity (§43).

## Key examples
- The pump and water supply, and poisoning for the sake of good government, as spatial and temporal distance; lying on the bed as resting, as a wider description (§41, as Elliott reports).
- The pumping example of §§23–26 invoked as the same order (§42).
- The stove burning vs paying the gas bill (§43).

## What it establishes / hands on
- **Practical reasoning = the order of intention.** The A–D order of "Why?" and the end-to-means order of calculation are one structure, read in opposite directions (Thompson 1998; Wiland & Driver; Frey).
- **Anti-psychologism.** The account is not of mental mechanisms (§42).
- Hence §33's promise can be kept. Practical knowledge (§§45, 48) is knowledge of this order as it is present in what one does. §43's complexity of human doing points to §§46–47, where "intentional" is a form of description of events.
- §44 (G14) considers conditional calculations ("if I do this, this will happen…") followed by action, and asks when they count as practical reasoning.

- **The order:**
  - [Thompson 1998](../../sources/secondary/thompson-1998-rep-anscombe.md): the "Why?" order is the reverse of the reasoning order.
  - [Wiland & Driver 2022](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md): the two are equivalent.
  - [Frey 2016](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md): first in intention, last in execution.
  - [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md): the agent knows the whole order without deliberating.
  - [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md): the "absurd appearance".
- **Anti-psychologism and structure:** [Millgram 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) (practical reasoning identical with intention's order; gas bill with §46). [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) pushes back: Anscombe still talks about individual agents.
- **Grouping:** [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) puts §41 with the guise of the good (5.4) and treats §§42–43 as 5.5 "an order which is there". This repo puts §41 here because its contents line is structural.
