# G14 — Practical knowledge and the problem of failure (§§44–45)

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part VI: Practical Knowledge](../L1-parts.md#part-vi-practical-knowledge) › **G14** · [← G13](G13-s41-43-the-order-of-practical-reasoning.md) · [G15 →](G15-s46-48-form-of-description-and-practical-knowledge.md)

## Where this group sits
Part IV (§§28–32) left a puzzle: the agent knows without observation what he is doing, yet what he is doing is a public happening also knowable by observation — must there then be two objects of knowledge (§29)? Anscombe sketched a solution (contradiction of intentions, §31; the shopping list and "mistake in performance", §32) but said that "practical knowledge" could only be understood once practical reasoning was (§33). Part V (§§33–43) supplied that account: practical reasoning displays an *order* of means and ends that is there whenever actions are done with intentions (§42). G14 is the hinge: §44 rounds off the practical-reasoning discussion, and §45 at last introduces practical knowledge by a model case — and immediately confronts the strongest objection to it.

## The argument, step by step
1. Action following a conditional calculation ("if I do this, this; if that, that") is not by that fact practical reasoning; whether it is depends on the role the calculation plays (§44).
2. Practical knowledge is introduced via a director who erects a building purely by orders, without sight or reports; settling things "in a right order", he knows *what is done* — unlike someone merely conceiving speculatively how it might be done (§45).
3. What he knows is, first of all, what the house is like; the parallel is writing "I am a fool" on a blackboard with eyes shut, knowing what one writes without looking (§45; cf. §29).
4. Objection: he knows only a conditional, and since orders can be disobeyed and intentions fail, his "knowledge" would have been the same whatever happened — "a funny sort of knowledge" (§45).
5. Response via Theophrastus: where intention is not executed, the mistake is in the performance, not the judgment (§45; cf. §§2, 32).
6. Coda: the temptation to make an "idea" the object of the will, and the conventional case where saying "It's yours!" makes it so (§45).
7. The problem is left to be taken up again in §48.

## Key examples
- The unseen builder directing by orders (§45).
- Writing on the blackboard with eyes shut; the chalk that fails (§45).
- "It's yours!" (§45).

## What it establishes / hands on
- The term and model of *practical knowledge*: knowledge of what is done, not of an inner state, not derived from observation (§45).
- A sharply posed problem — the possibility of failure — that §48 addresses with "the rare exception" and the "cause of what it understands".
- The Theophrastus point linking §2, §32 and §45, which §52 relies on when relating intention and prediction.

## Commentators on this stretch
- **Is §45's knowledge factive?** [Özaltun 2016](../../sources/secondary/ozaltun-2016-practical-knowledge-section-45.md) reads it strongly factively, assigning "would have been the same" to an interlocutor; [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) also keeps factivity, reading Theophrastus as locating blame and "would have been the same" as a modal claim about one state; [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) takes the passage to concede non-factivity and calls it "a mis-step"; [Setiya 2022 SEP](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md) leaves it open (her epistemology is "elusive").
- **What is the object?** Completed deeds as well as ongoing ones ([Setiya 2016](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md)); the process of meeting execution-conditions, on an imperfective reading ([Hubbs 2016](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md), after Thompson and Haddock); the whole means–end order ([Wiland & Driver 2022](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md)). [Sandis 2015](../../sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md) restricts the independence claim to practical knowledge proper, not idle reasoning.
- **Options and freedom.** [Millgram 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) reads §§44–45 as showing practical reasons do not compel ("logical freedom").
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) discusses §§44–45 with §48 under "The cause of what it understands" (headings only).
