# G15 — A form of description, and practical knowledge as "cause of what it understands" (§§46–48)

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part VI: Practical Knowledge](../L1-parts.md#part-vi-practical-knowledge) › **G15** · [← G14](G14-s44-45-practical-knowledge-and-failure.md) · [G16 →](G16-s49-voluntary-action.md)

## Where this group sits
§45 introduced practical knowledge as the director's knowledge of what is done, but left a problem: if orders can be disobeyed and intentions fail, how is this knowledge of what happens? G15 answers by changing the question from the agent's state to the *descriptions* under which events are known. It is the culmination of the book's account of intentional action and practical knowledge.

## The argument, step by step
1. Descriptions such as "building a house" or "writing on a blackboard" already employ the concept of human action, which is defined by the question "Why?" (§46; cf. §5).
2. "Intentional" therefore relates to a *form* of description of events — the form displayed by the "Why?" inquiry — and not to an extra property of events (§47; cf. §19).
3. Two lists show how pervasive the form is: descriptions that apply only to intentional doings (greeting, paying, hiring, marrying…) and descriptions that apply sometimes to intentional, sometimes to non-intentional happenings (offending, kicking, dropping, switching on…) (§47).
4. Animals have intentions, though they cannot express them (§47).
5. Many descriptions of human doings are thus formally descriptions of executed intentions (§48).
6. Where an event's description is of that type and the event really is an execution of intention, practical knowledge is, with Aquinas, "the cause of what it understands", unlike speculative knowledge derived from its objects (§48).
7. This is more than being a necessary condition: the agent's knowledge gives the descriptions under which what happens is the execution of an intention (§48).
8. Failure to execute intentions is necessarily the rare exception, though failure to attain one's further goods is common; the §45 builder is "very improbable" (§48).
9. Practical knowledge is related to know-how but is the exercise of a capacity in doing or supervising operations, and every intentional action presupposes "knowing one's way about" its matters (§48).

## Key examples
- The two lists of intention-dependent descriptions (§47).
- Animal intention (§47).
- Lecturing on a skill while being unable to exercise it; reciting the alphabet as a mere effect (§48).

## What it establishes / hands on
- The book's positive account of intentional action (form of description) and practical knowledge (formal relation of knowledge to its object), answering §§29–32 and §45.
- The basis for §49 (the voluntary, now definable via the intentional) and for §§50–52 (future intention falls under the same account; what is intended is a worldly happening).

## Commentators on this stretch
- **What intention is (§47).** [Schwenkler c.2020](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-c2020-316-interview.md): §47 is Anscombe's nearest answer to "what is intention?". [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) and [Wiland & Driver 2022](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md): the lists block the "extra property" view; W&D read §47 as the book's conclusion. Marcus (in [Haddock & Wiseman 2022](../../sources/secondary/haddock-wiseman-2022-anscombean-mind.md)) on animals.
- **Formal vs efficient cause (§48).** Formal: [Setiya 2016](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md), [Setiya 2022 SEP](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md), [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md), [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md), [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum 2023](../../sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md) (who note efficient causation is disputed). Formal *and* efficient: [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md). Efficient-reliabilist reading: Velleman 2007, as reported and rejected by Setiya.
- **Always or only sometimes?** Setiya 2016 argues the causality holds only under conditions (a) and (b) and names Moran, Stoutland, McDowell and Rödl as reading it unrestrictedly; [Frey 2016](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) and [Hubbs 2016](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md) adopt the Thomistic formula without that restriction.
- **Aquinas source.** *ST* I-II q. 3 a. 5 obj. 1 for the quoted phrase (Small; Hubbs; [Elliott 2016](../../sources/secondary/elliott-2016-anscombe-practical-truth.md)); *ST* I q. 14 (divine knowledge) as the background doctrine (Schwenkler c.2020).
- **Knowledge and know-how.** Small resolves a §28/§48 tension in favour of §48 (intentional action presupposes know-how); [Setiya 2008](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md) says the connection is undeveloped.
- **Does intentional action require knowledge?** Schwenkler (2019; c.2020): not strictly; McDowell vs Özaltun in [Haddock & Wiseman 2022](../../sources/secondary/haddock-wiseman-2022-anscombean-mind.md).
