# G17 — Intention for the future revisited (§§50–52)

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part VII: Voluntary Action and Intention for the Future](../L1-parts.md#part-vii-voluntary-action-and-intention-for-the-future) › **G17** · [← G16](G16-s49-voluntary-action.md)

## Where this group sits
The book began (§1) with three heads — expression of intention for the future, intentional action, intention in acting — and opened with the first (§§2–4), only to set it aside as offering no direct way into the concept. After the long investigation of intentional action, intention-with-which, practical reasoning and practical knowledge (§§5–48), and the account of the voluntary (§49), Anscombe returns to where she started. G17 is the book's closing frame: it shows that the first head needs no separate theory.

## The argument, step by step
1. The inquiry into intentional action and intention-with-which is complete; return to expressions of intention for the future (§50).
2. What has been said about intention in present action applies to future intention; a prediction is an expression of intention when the special "Why?" applies to it (§50).
3. The limiting answer "I just want to, that's all" to "Why are you going to…?" is examined, as the future analogue of "no particular reason"; it still invites questions of desirability (§51; cf. §17; [Millgram](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md)).
4. Can one say "I am not going to…" (intention) together with "I am going to…" (belief)? (§52).
5. Cases where they seem to coexist: the man on the precipice, the man facing torture, St Peter (§52).
6. Intention and prediction concern "the very same thing": what another knows will not happen is what I said would happen (§52).
7. The first-person combination nonetheless sounds contradictory where the third-person one does not — a Moore's-paradox-like structure, as commentators put it (§52).

## Key examples
- "I just want to, that's all" (§51).
- Precipice; torture; St Peter's denial (§52).
- Going for a walk, and the person who knows it will not happen (§52).

## What it establishes / hands on
- The unity of the three heads: future intention is understood through the account of intentional action and the "Why?" question, without positing intention as a separate mental state (§§50–52).
- The object of an expression of intention is a worldly happening, the same thing an observer may predict or deny (§52) — extending practical knowledge to the future.
- Ties the end of the book to §§2–3 (prediction in a wide sense; Theophrastus; justification).

## Commentators on this stretch
- **Unity without a mental state.** [Setiya 2022 SEP](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md): §§50–52 show future intention needs no separate account; Anscombe solves unity without intention as a mental state — a line developed by Thompson, Moran & Stone and Falvey, which Setiya criticises. [Thompson 1998](../../sources/secondary/thompson-1998-rep-anscombe.md): future intention derivative; "Why?" applies to first-person future statements. Moran & Stone's exegesis covers §§2–4, 22, 50–52 ([Ford, Hornsby & Stoutland 2011](../../sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md); not read).
- **Practical foreknowledge.** [Setiya 2016](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md) (p. 92 "very same thing"; against Thompson's failure-tolerant "going to"); [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md); [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum 2023](../../sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md) (§§51–52 vs imperfective-only readings); [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md), [Campbell 2024](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2024-practical-knowledge-structural-challenge.md).
- **Architecture and Moore's paradox.** [Millgram 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md): §§2, 50–52 frame the book; intentions and predictions not "head-on" contradictories (with the §22 caveat); determinism and the freedom of action "pass each other by". [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md): parallel with Wittgenstein on Moore's paradox; "factualism". [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md): the §52 cases; Peter's ignorance of *how*; intention-with-which runs to §50.
- **Extent.** Holton puts the end of the intention-with-which discussion at §50, against Wiseman's §40 (as Holton reports it).
