# §05 — Intentional action defined by a sense of the question "Why?"

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part II: The Question "Why?"](../L1-parts.md#part-ii-the-question-why) › [G03: §§5–8](../L2-sections/G03-s05-08-why-question-and-its-refusal.md) › **§05** · [← §04](§04.md) · [§06 →](§06.md)

*Pages: from p. 9 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: intentional actions are defined as those to which a certain sense of the question "Why?" is given application; it is hard to define that sense, and there is a danger of circularity in explaining "reason for acting" and "action"; "I knocked the cup off the table because I was startled" answers a "Why?" about something that was done.*

## In one sentence
Anscombe proposes that intentional actions are those to which a special sense of "Why?" applies — the sense in which the answer, if positive, gives a reason for acting — and immediately recognises that she cannot specify that sense by appeal to "reasons for acting" without circularity.

## Summary
Turning from intention for the future to intentional action (§4 → §5), Anscombe gives her famous definition: intentional actions are those to which a certain sense of the question "Why?" is given application, the sense in which the answer, if positive, gives a reason for acting (§5). The difficulty is to say *which* sense this is. Explaining it as "the sense that asks for a reason for acting" would be circular, because the notion of a reason for acting is no clearer than that of an action: we would need to explain why, for instance, giving a start or a gasp is not an action while sending for a taxi or crossing the road is ([Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md), on §5). The contents line's example shows the problem from the other side: "I knocked the cup off the table because I was startled" is a perfectly good answer to a "Why?" asked about something that was done, yet it gives a cause, not a reason (§5). So "Why?" has several senses — commentators distinguish evidential, causal and reason-requesting ones ([Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md)) — and the reason-requesting sense must be isolated without presupposing what it is meant to explain. Anscombe's strategy, carried out in §§6–8, is negative first: identify the answers that show the question in this sense to be *refused application* (§§6–8), then (§§9–18) chart the answers that grant it.

## Main claim(s)
- An action is intentional iff a certain sense of "Why?" has application to it (§5).
- That sense cannot be defined simply as "the request for a reason for acting" without circularity (§5).
- A "Why?" can be asked and answered about something done without the answer giving a reason (startle case) (§5).

## Examples
- "I knocked the cup off the table because I was startled" (§5, contents line).
- Giving a start or a gasp (not actions) versus sending for a taxi or crossing the road (actions) (§5, per Small).

## Objections and alternatives Anscombe considers
- Defining intentional action via "reason for acting" (circular) (§5).
- Treating any answer to "Why?" about a doing as showing intentionality (refuted by the startle case) (§5).

## In the secondary literature
- [Thompson 1998](../../sources/secondary/thompson-1998-rep-anscombe.md) — takes intentional action as prior and the "Why?" test as the task of isolating a form of explanation.
- [Setiya 2022](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md) — p. 9 cited: acting on a ground requires the applicability of "Why?" — used against Bratman-style planning theory; [Setiya 2008](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md) — takes from p. 9 that what we do for reasons we do intentionally; [Setiya 2016](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md) — descriptions answering "Why?" are formally descriptions of executed intentions (§47).
- [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) — three senses of "Why?" (evidential, causal, reason-giving, his examples); glossing the definition as "request for reasons" is too tight to illuminate.
- [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) — warns against the popular attribution "intentional iff done for reasons": §5's criterion leaves room for action for no reason (§§17–18), and §5 itself flags the circularity.
- [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum 2023](../../sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md) — "Why?" reveals the rational means–end order later linked to practical reasoning (§33 ff.); Anscombe's special "Why?" is something Davidson does not deny.
- [Schlosser 2019](../../sources/secondary/schlosser-2019-sep-agency.md) — *divergence/caution*: identifies intentional action with acting for a reason rationalisable by a sound practical syllogism, which sits badly with §17 and §35.
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) — Ch. 2.1 asks why Anscombe defines intentional action by a sense of "Why?"; separates reason-giving from cause-identifying explanation.
- [Wiseman 2025](../../sources/secondary/wiseman-2025-philosophy-talk.md) — "Why?" as the everyday linguistic tool for finding intentions, whose many answers display the concept's structure.
- [Richter n.d.](../../sources/secondary/richter-nd-iep-anscombe.md) — gloss with his own example: tossing in one's sleep admits only a causal "why", which anyone could answer as well as the sleeper.
- [Ford, Hornsby & Stoutland 2011](../../sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md) — Laurence extends the "Why?" to a collective sense ("We're pushing it to the gas station").
- [Haddock & Wiseman 2022](../../sources/secondary/haddock-wiseman-2022-anscombean-mind.md) — Marcus (§§5–6, §47) on whether answerability to "Why?" restricts intentional action to language users; Mac Cumhaill on "Why?" and sensation.
- [Speaks 2004 (Davidson notes)](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2004-davidson-actions-reasons-causes.md) — presents Davidson's starting point, the equivalence "A φs intentionally ≡ A φs for a reason" that "many take" to hold (not attributed to Anscombe); compare §§17–18 on "for no particular reason".
