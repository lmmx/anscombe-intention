# §06 — First refusal: "I was not aware I was doing that"

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part II: The Question "Why?"](../L1-parts.md#part-ii-the-question-why) › [G03: §§5–8](../L2-sections/G03-s05-08-why-question-and-its-refusal.md) › **§06** · [← §05](§05.md) · [§07 →](§07.md)

*Pages: from p. 11 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: the question "Why?" is refused application by the answer "I did not know I was doing that"; one and the same action can have many descriptions, under some of which the agent knows it and under others not.*

## In one sentence
The special "Why?" is shown not to apply when the agent sincerely answers that he did not know he was doing what he is asked about — which, since one action has many descriptions, makes intentionality relative to description.

## Summary
Beginning the negative route announced in §5, Anscombe identifies a first answer that does not answer the question but rejects it: "I was not aware I was doing that" (§6). If that answer is true, the "Why?" in the relevant sense has no application to the action *as described*. This makes knowledge of what one is doing a necessary condition of acting intentionally under a description — what Setiya calls Anscombe's "bold assumption" (p. 11) ([Setiya 2008](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md)) and Campbell the "knowledge condition" ([Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md)).

The section's second point is that a single action may have many descriptions, and the agent may know it under some of them but not others (§6, contents). A man sawing a plank may know he is sawing a plank without knowing he is sawing *Smith's* plank, or that he is making a squeaky noise with the saw (§6, as reported by [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) and [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md)). He saws the plank intentionally; he does not intentionally saw Smith's plank. So "intentional" attaches to an action *under a description*, not to an event simpliciter — a thesis later developed in §§23–26 and §47. The refusal thus gives the first of the epistemic conditions on intentional action (Schwenkler's C1).

## Main claim(s)
- "I didn't know I was doing that", if true, shows "Why?" (in the relevant sense) has no application (§6).
- The same action can be described in many ways; the agent may know it under some descriptions and not others (§6).
- Hence intentionality is relative to description (§6; developed in §§23–26).

## Examples
- Sawing a plank / sawing Smith's plank / making a squeaky noise with the saw (§6, per Holton and Small).

## In the secondary literature
- [Setiya 2008](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md) — the "bold assumption" (p. 11) that one cannot be φ-ing intentionally without knowing it; Setiya himself weakens it (knowledge not strictly necessary, Davidson's carbon-copier).
- [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md) — not knowing that one is doing something under a description is generally sufficient for not doing it intentionally (I, 11); contrapositively, intentional action requires knowledge; notes carbon-copier objection and replies (Thompson 2011, Wolfson 2012).
- [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) — strengthens the refusal to "lacks *non-observational* knowledge" (his physical-therapy mirror case); counterexamples: the professor who insults unwittingly; making ten carbon copies (Davidson's, uncredited); falls back to Setiya's weaker principle.
- [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) — the relata of the "Why?" test are actions under descriptions; classes this refusal as (A)(i), ignorance.
- [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) — "I didn't know I was X-ing" as one of two key refusals (with §8).
- [Wiseman 2025](../../sources/secondary/wiseman-2025-philosophy-talk.md) — everyday parallel: someone barges past and says she didn't see you; at once we know "barging into this guy" was not a description under which she acted.
- [Frey 2016](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) — refusal ("I didn't know he was in here") shows the description is not intentional.
- [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum 2023](../../sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md) — cite "I was not aware I was doing that" as §6 *and* §42; the §42 citation is a probable mis-citation (§42 concerns the order of practical reasoning).
- [Haddock & Wiseman 2022](../../sources/secondary/haddock-wiseman-2022-anscombean-mind.md) — Wiseman's chapter treats "Why?" and the "description under which" (§§6, 23–26) by analogy with sensation.
- [Teichmann (ed.) 2022](../../sources/secondary/teichmann-2022-oxford-handbook-anscombe.md) — Clot-Goudard: intentional under a description only with knowledge under that description, hence ignorance can excuse, though it can be culpable.
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) — §§6–8 as three epistemic conditions C1–C3; later concludes knowledge is not strictly required for intentional action (Ch. 6).

## Notes and uncertainties
- **Edition variant (1963)** per [Sandis 2015](../../sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md): "To justify the proposed account … I need to explain" became "To clarify the proposed account … I will … explain" — Sandis reads this as a considerable weakening (defence → clarification).
- † = writer's recollection of the text, not corroborated by the contents line or a saved source; check against the book. The † in "Objections" concerns only how the justify/clarify sentence functions in context, not the variant itself.
- Whether Anscombe herself uses the exact phrase "under a description" in §6 is not verified; the contents line speaks of an action having many descriptions.
