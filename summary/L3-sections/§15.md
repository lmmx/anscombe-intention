# §15 — Where reason and mental cause are not sharply distinct

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part II: The Question "Why?"](../L1-parts.md#part-ii-the-question-why) › [G05: §§12–15](../L2-sections/G05-s12-15-motives.md) › **§15** · [← §14](§14.md) · [§16 →](§16.md)

*Pages: from p. 23 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: in some cases the distinction between a mental cause and a reason is not sharp, e.g. "I put it down because he told me to".*

## In one sentence
Anscombe concedes that the line between mental cause and reason is not everywhere sharp, using the example of doing something because one was told to.

## Summary
After drawing distinctions in §§10–14, Anscombe admits a range of cases where they blur (§15). Her example is "I put it down because he told me to" (§15, contents line). Being told can function like a mental cause — the order simply *made* one do it, much as a noise makes one jump — or like a reason, where the agent treats the order as a ground, something whose being said bears on what is good to do (cf. §14 on good and harm). In many such cases there is no determinate fact settling which it was, and nothing hangs on forcing a decision. Holton cites Anscombe's remark that reason and cause are not "everywhere sharply distinct" (p. 24) ([Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md)). †The more the answer resembles "it made me", and the less any thought of the act as a fitting response is involved, the more it is like a cause; the more such thought is involved, the more it is a reason. The concession does not undo the distinctions: there remain clear cases of each, and cases where one item is both ([Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md)).

## Main claim(s)
- The distinction between mental cause and reason is not always sharp (§15).
- "Because he told me to" can fall on either side, or neither determinately (§15).

## Examples
- "I put it down because he told me to" (§15).

## Objections and alternatives Anscombe considers
- That the cause/reason distinction, to be valid, must be sharp in every case (implicitly rejected) (§15).

## In the secondary literature
- [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) — §15 "muddies" the picture: often it seems arbitrary whether to call something reason or cause; plausible reading: clear reason-cases, clear cause-cases, and cases where one item is both.
- [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) — reason and cause not "everywhere sharply distinct" (p. 24); evidence that Anscombe is not much concerned to establish that reasons exclude causes.
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) — Ch. 2.3 on reason, motive and cause (§§9–16); against reading Anscombe as an anti-causalist.
