*Pages: from p. 24 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: a summary of the results reached so far.*

## In one sentence
Anscombe gathers §§5–15 into a statement of when the special "Why?" is refused application and of the kinds of answer that grant it.

## Summary
A short recapitulation (§16). Intentional actions are a sub-class of things known without observation, picked out as those to which the special "Why?" applies. The question is refused application when the agent (i) did not know he was doing the thing under the description given (§6); (ii) knew it only by observation (§8); or (iii) knew it without observation but knew its cause only by observation, or the answer gives evidence or a cause — including a mental cause (§§8–11) ([Wiland & Driver 2022](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md), who cite p. 24; [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md)). Positively, where the question has application, the answer may (a) mention something past whose relevance depends on its being taken as good or harm (backward-looking motive, §§13–14); (b) give an interpretation of the action (motive-in-general, §13); or (c) mention something future (further intention, §§11–12) (§16, per Speaks and Holton). Wiland & Driver add "No reason" (§17) to the list of answers that grant application.

## Main claim(s)
- Refusals: ignorance; knowledge only by observation; evidence or (mental) cause as the only available answer (§16).
- Positive answer-types: past history taken as good/harm; interpretation; something future (§16).

## Examples
- Wiland & Driver's sample answers (theirs, not quoted from Anscombe): "To end the war" (future), "Out of curiosity" (interpretation), "They attacked us first" (past ground).

## In the secondary literature
- [Wiland & Driver 2022](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md) — cite INT 24 as Anscombe's disjunctive first answer; call it a "hodgepodge" that says little about intention itself, after which she "shifts methods" (reading the book backwards from §47).
- [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) — extracts three criteria: non-observational knowledge that and why; "Why?" not a request for evidence or cause; answers (a) past history, (b) interpretation, (c) future.
- [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) — quotes §16 in full. *Divergence*: his taxonomy places mental-cause cases under "the question gets application", whereas §16 counts an answer giving a mental cause among those that do not grant it (flagged in the source evaluation).
- [Schlosser 2019](../../sources/secondary/schlosser-2019-sep-agency.md) — compresses §§5, 16–18 into "intentional action = action for a reason"; flagged as a simplification at odds with §17.
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) — §16 closes the "reason, motive and cause" subsection (§§9–16).
