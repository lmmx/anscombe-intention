# §17 — "For no particular reason" and "I don't know why I did it" do not refuse the question

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part II: The Question "Why?"](../L1-parts.md#part-ii-the-question-why) › [G06: §§16–18](../L2-sections/G06-s16-18-the-range-of-answers.md) › **§17** · [← §16](§16.md) · [§18 →](§18.md)

*Pages: from p. 25 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: "Why?" is not refused application when the answer is "For no particular reason" or "I don't know why I did it"; the latter is considered.*

## In one sentence
An answer saying there was no reason, or that one doesn't know why one did it, still accepts the question's application — it is a nil answer, not a rejection — so intentional action does not require a reason.

## Summary
Anscombe distinguishes answers that *reject* the question (§§6–8: "I didn't know I was doing that", etc.) from answers that *accept* it but supply nothing substantive (§17). "For no particular reason", "No reason", "I just thought I would" belong to the second kind: they no more refuse application than answering "None" refuses the question how much money I have in my pocket — as opposed to "I have no pockets" ([Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md); [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md)). Hence one can act intentionally for no reason, and the "Why?" criterion of §5 does not equate intentional action with action for a reason ([Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md); [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md)).

The second answer, "I don't know why I did it", gets separate consideration (§17, contents line). †It is typically said by someone who knows perfectly well what he did, and is not reporting ignorance of a cause in the way "I don't know what made me jump" might; it expresses puzzlement about one's act, often with a sense that there might be some explanation to be found. Such an answer, too, leaves the question applicable. §18 then asks whether "For no particular reason" always makes sense.

## Main claim(s)
- "For no particular reason" does not refuse application of "Why?" (§17).
- "I don't know why I did it" likewise does not refuse application (§17).
- So intentional action can be action for no reason (§17; qualified in §§18, 20).

## Examples
- "None" as an answer to "How much money is in your pocket?" vs. having no pockets (§17, per Holton and Small).

## Objections and alternatives Anscombe considers
- That a nil answer shows the question inapplicable (rejected) (§17).

## In the secondary literature
- [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) — quotes §17 with the pocket analogy; places it under "intentional acts" (question gets application).
- [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) — the question applies with a negative answer; used against the attribution "intentional iff done for reasons".
- [Setiya 2022](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md) — idle action "for no particular reason" (p. 25) is a difficulty for unifying the guises by reducing intentional action to intention-with-which.
- [Wiland & Driver 2022](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md) — count "No reason" among answers that grant application.
- [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md) — on her view acts "for no particular reason" (I, 25) manifest neither practical truth nor practical falsity in the ethical sense.
- [Frey 2016](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) — acting for no reason as secondary cases (§§17–18, 20).
- [Schlosser 2019](../../sources/secondary/schlosser-2019-sep-agency.md) — *divergence*: identifies intentional action with acting for a reason, which §17 contradicts.
- [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) — a question's having application is not its having a substantive answer; uses this to object to §20.
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) — Ch. 2.4 on acting "for no reason" or for reasons one cannot identify (§§17–18).
- [Millgram 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) — "Oh, no reason" answers mark a limit of the pumping model (§§17–18, 20–21).

## Notes and uncertainties
- **Edition variant (1963)**: "has no application" → "is shewn not to apply" — judged purely stylistic by [Sandis 2015](../../sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md).
- † = writer's recollection of the text, not corroborated by the contents line or a saved source; check against the book. Applies to the characterisation of "I don't know why I did it".
