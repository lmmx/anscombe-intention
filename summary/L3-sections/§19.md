# §19 — "Intentional" names no extra feature of the action

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part III: The Unity of Action](../L1-parts.md#part-iii-the-unity-of-action) › [G07: §§19–21](../L2-sections/G07-s19-21-no-extra-feature-and-chains-of-reasons.md) › **§19** · [← §18](§18.md) · [§20 →](§20.md)

*Pages: from p. 28 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: to call an action intentional is not to mention some additional feature belonging to it at the time it is done; this is shown by supposing that there is such a feature and seeing what follows.*

## In one sentence
Having fixed the class of intentional actions by the range of answers to the special "Why?" (§§5–18), Anscombe argues that calling an action intentional assigns it to that class rather than reporting some further item (an inner act, feeling or state) present when it was done.

## Summary
§18 closed Part II by identifying the special "Why?" as the question that expects an answer in the described range, and that range as what defines the class of intentional actions. §19 draws the moral: the word "intentional" does not pick out an extra feature that accompanies the movement at the time it occurs (contents line). Anscombe proves this by *reductio*: suppose there were such a feature — an inner occurrence present whenever an action is intentional (contents line).

The difficulty she presses, as reconstructed by [Speaks](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md), is that actions are intentional only *under a description* (§6), whereas an occurrence in the agent at the time stands only in a contingent relation to whether a given description is satisfied. If the feature were what made the action intentional, its connection with the right description would be a "happy accident", unless some further mechanism ensured that the right inner item occurred because of what the agent knew. Per [Sandis 2015](../../sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md), the example in this passage concerns how a supposed inner item would have to be linked to what the movement will result in; in the 1963 text it is that the agent's muscular contractions will result in his grasping a hammer. Such a feature would therefore be idle: the occurrence of an inner "intending" could not by itself fix under which description the action was intentional. The conclusion is that "intentional" characterises the action by its belonging to a class marked out by the applicability of "Why?" — a classification, not the report of a further ingredient. [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) quotes the sentence: to call an action intentional is to assign it to the class of intentional actions and so to indicate that the question "Why?" is relevant to it.

The section is short but programmatic: it sets up the claims that intention is not a separable mental accompaniment of action (developed in §27 on interior acts, §§29–30 on acts of will and initiating events, and §47, where "intentional" is said to relate to a *form of description* of events and not to an extra property).

## Main claim(s)
- Being intentional is not a matter of some additional feature existing at the time of acting (§19, contents line).
- To call an action intentional is to place it in the class to which the special "Why?" applies (§§18–19, per Speaks).
- Any postulated inner feature would relate only contingently to the description under which the action is intentional (Speaks's reconstruction).

## Examples
- The hammer-grasping example (inner item vs. what the bodily process results in); its wording differs between printings (see Notes) — [Sandis 2015](../../sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md).

## Objections and alternatives Anscombe considers
- The view that intentional action is a bodily movement plus an accompanying inner feature (intention, act of will, feeling) — rejected by the reductio (contents line).

## Cross-references within *Intention*

## In the secondary literature
- [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) — calls it a "surprising claim"; finds the slogan uninformative if read literally and reconstructs it as a metaphysical thesis (no facts of a certain sort make actions intentional) via a contingency argument; judges the argument effective against *intrinsic, physical* features but of limited scope beyond them; leaves open whether an intention causing a movement "in the right way" escapes it.
- [Ford–Hornsby–Stoutland 2011](../../sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md) — Ford ("Action and Generality") reads §19 as rejecting the genus-plus-differentia method in action theory: action stands to intentional action as gold to pure gold, nothing extra added (as reported by Clark and Piedrahita).
- [Millgram 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) — finds the "nothing added" argument (§19's second paragraph) weak taken piecemeal (Bratman would be unmoved), but read as a frame around the pumping model it shows intention to be the *formal cause* of an action, internal to it; cites Vogler 2019, "Nothing Added: Intention §§19 & 20".
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) — Ch. 3.1 "An extra feature?" (§19); concludes that calling an action intentional specifies the kind of description it falls under rather than adding a feature (per Pellegrini's review).
- [Setiya 2016](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md) — identifies the "extra feature" view as the target of §§47–48 (where it recurs), including belief–desire and intention-causal analyses.
- [Sandis 2015](../../sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md) — records the 1958 change on p. 29 (see Notes) and reads it as evidence that Anscombe did not count muscle contractions as the outer bodily movement under another description.
- [Wikipedia 2026](../../sources/secondary/wikipedia-2026-anscombe-intention-section.md) — cites "sec. 18–21" for the centrality of reason-giving answers; low reliability.
- **Flagged citations.** [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum 2023](../../sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md) cite §19 for practical knowledge of "what is written"; this does not match the contents line and is unverified. [Campbell 2024](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2024-practical-knowledge-structural-challenge.md) quotes Frost citing "2000, p. 29" for there being no single privileged description of an action; p. 29 falls inside §19, whereas the contents line puts "THE description" in §23 (p. 37) — check.

## Notes and uncertainties
- **Edition variant (1958 impression).** Per [Sandis 2015](../../sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md), the 1957 first edition's example "the movement of his hand will result in its grasping the hammer" became, from 1958, "his muscular contractions will result in his grasping the hammer" (p. 29). Anscombe listed p. 29 among the few significant alterations. Quote the 1963/2000 wording.
- **Verification pass:** the final step of the Summary (intentional = classification by the applicability of "Why?"), formerly marked †, is corroborated by Speaks's quotation of §19 ([Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md)). No † items remain. The exact shape of Anscombe's reductio is reconstructed from Speaks and Sandis, not from the text.
