# §20 — Intentional action depends on further intention and intention for the future

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part III: The Unity of Action](../L1-parts.md#part-iii-the-unity-of-action) › [G07: §§19–21](../L2-sections/G07-s19-21-no-extra-feature-and-chains-of-reasons.md) › **§20** · [← §19](§19.md) · [§21 →](§21.md)

*Pages: from p. 30 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: could intentional actions still be intentional if there were no such thing as expressing intention for the future, or acting with a further intention? If "For no particular reason" were the only possible answer, there would be no "Why?" in the relevant sense, and so no intentional action.*

## In one sentence
Intentional action, which Part II treated as the most tractable of the three heads, turns out not to be conceptually self-standing: a practice in which the only answer to "Why?" was "For no particular reason" would contain no intentional actions at all.

## Summary
§1 distinguished three heads — expression of intention for the future, intentional action, intention with which one acts. Part II isolated intentional action via the "Why?" question and allowed that the question may have application even when the answer is "For no particular reason" (§17). §20 asks whether intentional action could exist on its own: suppose (a) there were no expressions of intention for the future, and (b) no expressions of further intention in acting (Schwenkler's labels for the two suppositions; contents line).

Anscombe's answer is negative. Two lines of argument are reported in the sources:
1. **Against a "style" model.** One might think "intentional" characterises an action as "sad" characterises a face — a recognisable cast of the movement itself. But an action is intentional under some descriptions and not others (§6), which is unlike a face's being sad ([Speaks](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md)).
2. **The idle "Why?".** If the only answer ever given were "For no particular reason" (or "I just did"), the question would not have the special sense at all, and intentional actions could not be distinguished from involuntary movements known without observation (Speaks's example: a gasp). The "Why?" that defines intentional action presupposes that *some* answers mention a further intention or something future (contents line).

Along the way Anscombe considers marking intentional actions by their being the sort of thing that can be commanded. [Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) highlights the "Tremble" example — trembling in response to a terrifying voice that commands "Tremble!" is not obeying — as an early recognition of deviant causation; [Hubbs](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md) reports that the commandability criterion is judged "idle". The upshot is that the three heads are interdependent: the concept of intentional action lives inside a practice that includes stating further intentions and intentions for the future.

## Main claim(s)
- Intentional action is not intelligible independently of intention with which and expression of intention for the future (§20, contents line).
- "For no particular reason" is a possible answer only against a background in which other answers are available (§§17, 20).
- Being intentional is not a perceptible "cast" of a movement (Speaks's "sad face" reconstruction).

## Examples
- The sad face, as a rejected model of "intentional" ([Speaks](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md)).
- "Tremble!": trembling caused by a commanding voice is not obedience ([Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md)).
- Involuntary movements known without observation (Speaks's gasp; cf. §8), with which intentional action would be indistinguishable.

## Objections and alternatives Anscombe considers
- That "intentional" might be a quality of the movement, like a facial expression (rejected).
- That intentional actions might be marked off as what can be commanded (judged idle, per Hubbs).
- † That a people might have a concept of intentional action with only "no particular reason" answers — the imagined single-head practice Holton refers to (reported by Holton; exact construction not in sources).

## In the secondary literature
- [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) — reconstructs two arguments (style model; indistinguishability from involuntary movements). Objection: Anscombe allows intentional action for no reason, so why not a possible world where that is the only kind? At most no one there would have the *concept*, which is not the same as there being no intentional actions.
- [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) — "Tremble" as prescient on deviant causal chains; Anscombe's method of imagining concepts that apply under one head only, to show why the heads hang together.
- [Hubbs 2016](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md) — counts §20 among the intention/command parallels (§2, §§31–32, §45); commandability as a mark of the intentional is "idle" (n. 7).
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) — Ch. 3.2 "Further intention" (§§20–21): intentional action depends on the practices of expressing intention for the future and stating further intentions (OUP abstract).
- [Millgram 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) — §§20–21 (not everything can be "for no particular reason") parallel Davidson's anti-sceptical point that one cannot be wrong about most things; cites Vogler 2019 on §§19–20.
- [Frey 2016](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) — treats acting for no reason (§§17–18, 20) as secondary, parasitic cases.
- [Wikipedia 2026](../../sources/secondary/wikipedia-2026-anscombe-intention-section.md) — cites "sec. 18–21" for reason-giving answers being crucial to the phenomenon; low reliability but fair here.

## Notes and uncertainties
- The section is sometimes read (Speaks) as a claim about the concept and sometimes (Holton, Schwenkler) as a claim about the practice in which the concept lives; the contents line ("there would be no 'Why?' or intentional action") supports a claim about both.
- † = writer's recollection of the text, not corroborated by the contents line or a saved source; check against the book. The order in which the two arguments and the "Tremble" example occur within §20 is not fixed by the sources.
- See [glossary](../../glossary.md) ("intention with which", "For no particular reason").
