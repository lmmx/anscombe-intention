# §23 — Is there THE description of an action? The man at the pump

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part III: The Unity of Action](../L1-parts.md#part-iii-the-unity-of-action) › [G08: §§22–27](../L2-sections/G08-s22-27-intention-with-which-and-the-a-d-order.md) › **§23** · [← §22](§22.md) · [§24 →](§24.md)

*Pages: from p. 37 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: is any one description *the* description of an intentional action? An invented example: a man moving his arm up and down, operating a pump, replenishing a house's water-supply, poisoning its inhabitants — and doing other things with the pump handle as well. Every true description meeting the criteria is a description of an intentional action. Are there then as many actions and intentions as there are descriptions?*

## In one sentence
Through the invented case of a man pumping poisoned water into a house, Anscombe shows that one stretch of activity has many true descriptions, that those meeting the "Why?" criteria are all descriptions of intentional action, and that this raises the question how many actions and intentions there are.

## Summary
The section asks whether an intentional action has a privileged description (contents line). Anscombe invents a case. A man is moving his arm up and down; he is thereby operating a pump; the pump is replenishing the water-supply of a house; and, because the water source has been poisoned (by someone else, per the summary of [Wiseman 2025](../../sources/secondary/wiseman-2025-philosophy-talk.md)), he is poisoning the inhabitants, who are party chiefs (same source; [Millgram](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) says the case evokes an assassination of Nazi leaders). He is also doing many other things: contracting muscles, clicking out a rhythm with the handle, casting a shadow, doing his job ([Wiland & Driver](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md); [Wikipedia](../../sources/secondary/wikipedia-2026-anscombe-intention-section.md)).

Which descriptions are of intentional action? Anscombe's answer is that any true description satisfying the criteria of Part II — the agent knows without observation that he is doing it, and "Why?" has application — is a description of an intentional action (contents line). Others (e.g. contracting particular muscles, or an effect he does not know of) are not. "Why?" can be asked of each intentional description, and the answer typically gives the next, wider one: he moves his arm *because* he is operating the pump, operates it to replenish the supply, and so on. The descriptions are linked by "by" and "in order to" (Speaks).

Two further observations reported from §23:
- **Doing vs. going to do.** As answers to "Why are you doing B?", "I am going to do A", "I am doing A" and "in order to do A" are to an extent interchangeable — e.g. going upstairs to fetch a camera ([Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md); [Setiya SEP](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md), pp. 39–40). The line between "He is Y-ing" and "He is going to Y" is not sharp; the less one can take success as a matter of course, the more the objective is expressed only by "in order to" (Holton's example: going to London to make an uncle change his will, not "I am making my uncle change his will").
- **The question left open.** If every such description is a description of an intentional action, are there as many actions — and as many intentions — as descriptions (contents line)? The answer is deferred to §26, after difficulties in §§24–25.

## Main claim(s)
- No single description is *the* description of an intentional action (contents line).
- Any true description meeting the criteria of §§5–18 is a description of an intentional action (§23).
- The intentional descriptions form a series linked by "Why?"/"in order to" (§23; developed §26).
- Present-progressive, "going to" and "in order to" answers are partly interchangeable (§23, per Holton and Setiya).

## Examples
- The pump: arm-moving → operating the pump → replenishing the water-supply → poisoning the inhabitants; side-descriptions (muscle contractions, rhythmic clicking, shadow, doing his job).
- Going upstairs to fetch a camera ([Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md)).
- Going to London in order to make an uncle change his will ([Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md)).

## Objections and alternatives Anscombe considers
- That one description captures what the agent "really" does.
- That multiplicity of descriptions implies a multiplicity of actions (posed, answered in §26).

## In the secondary literature
- [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) — quotes the scenario; treats the three heads' equivalence "to a certain extent" (camera example) as the *conclusion* of Anscombe's inquiry; break points in the chain.
- [Setiya SEP 2022](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md) — pp. 39–40: no sharp line between "I am doing A" and "I am going to do A"; evidence that Anscombe unifies the heads without positing intention as a mental state.
- [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) — reads §§23–26 as answering: when is a description intentional, how are descriptions related, and one action or many? Uses his own typing example rather than the pump; finds the role of "circumstances" hard to pin down.
- [Wiland & Driver 2022](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md) — the pump's many descriptions; the "because, not merely and" ordering as the order of intention.
- [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum 2023](../../sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md) — the pump as showing a rational means–end order; adds an end "to bring world peace" (a loose paraphrase); suggests the progressive ("I am moving the lever because I am pumping because I am poisoning") favours a process ontology.
- [Millgram 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) — the "pumping model" (matryoshka nesting) as the template of the whole book; "the why is the what".
- [Millgram & Bowman 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-bowman-2026-sep-practical-reason-structure-of-actions.md) — the pump as the book's "centerpiece"; attribute to Anscombe the label "preintentional" for muscle activity below the arm movement (wording unverified).
- [Thompson 1998](../../sources/secondary/thompson-1998-rep-anscombe.md) — the "Why?" series reveals an order among descriptions of one action (his own Oedipus example for "under a description").
- [Wilson & Shpall 2012](../../sources/secondary/wilson-shpall-2012-sep-action.md); [Schlosser 2019](../../sources/secondary/schlosser-2019-sep-agency.md) — the "Davidson/Anscombe thesis" of one action under many descriptions (no § cited).
- [Elliott 2016](../../sources/secondary/elliott-2016-anscombe-practical-truth.md) — the pump as an example of spatial "distance" between action and thing wanted (cf. §41).
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) — treats §§22–23 with §26 (Ch. 3.3); his later broken-cisterns variant of the pump is cited by Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum as a case where "Why?" applies but belief is unsafe.
- **Divergence.** [Wiseman 2025](../../sources/secondary/wiseman-2025-philosophy-talk.md) records radio hosts' loose retellings (water "into a well", "killing Nazis"); not Anscombe's text.
