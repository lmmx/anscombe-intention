# §24 — When is he poisoning them? Difficulties for the many-descriptions view

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part III: The Unity of Action](../L1-parts.md#part-iii-the-unity-of-action) › [G08: §§22–27](../L2-sections/G08-s22-27-intention-with-which-and-the-a-d-order.md) › **§24** · [← §23](§23.md) · [§25 →](§25.md)

*Pages: from p. 41 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination; §25 also begins on p. 41, so §24 is very short). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: difficulties — if "poisoning the inhabitants" is one description of what he does, when is he doing it? And how can moving his arm up and down be an act of poisoning?*

## In one sentence
A brief statement of puzzles for §23's claim that arm-moving and poisoning are descriptions of one intentional action: the poisoning seems to occur later, and arm-moving seems quite a different thing from poisoning.

## Summary
§23 counted every true description meeting the criteria as a description of an intentional action, and asked whether there were as many actions as descriptions. §24 states difficulties for the natural answer that there is a single action (contents line):
1. **Timing.** The inhabitants are poisoned only when they drink the water, which may be long after the pumping. If "poisoning the inhabitants" describes what the pumper does, when is he doing it?
2. **Identity.** How can moving one's arm up and down *be* an act of poisoning, given how different the descriptions are?

These are posed, not resolved, here; the resolution comes in §26, where the descriptions are ordered as an A–D series of one action, each wider description depending on further circumstances. In §26 there is one action under a series of descriptions, not several actions, and the further descriptions apply in virtue of circumstances ([Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) on §26). Each description depends on the one below, and only more circumstances are required for A to be B, B to be C, and so on (McGinn; see Notes). So moving the arm, given those circumstances, is poisoning the inhabitants, with no second act occurring later.

## Main claim(s)
- The many-descriptions view faces questions of timing and identity (§24, contents line).

## Examples
- The pumper of §23 under the description "poisoning the inhabitants".

## Objections and alternatives Anscombe considers
- That arm-moving and poisoning must be distinct actions, because they occur at different times or are too unlike (stated as difficulties).
