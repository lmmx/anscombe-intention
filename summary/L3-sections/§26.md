# §26 — The A–D order: one action, a series of descriptions

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part III: The Unity of Action](../L1-parts.md#part-iii-the-unity-of-action) › [G08: §§22–27](../L2-sections/G08-s22-27-intention-with-which-and-the-a-d-order.md) › **§26** · [← §25](§25.md) · [§27 →](§27.md)

*Pages: from p. 45 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: the answer to §23 is the A–D order — an order among the descriptions of an action as intentional, in which each term is an intention in the action as described by the previous term, and the last term is the intention of the action as described by the first or any intermediate term.*

## In one sentence
Anscombe answers §23: the pumper performs one action under a series of descriptions A–D (moving his arm, operating the pump, replenishing the water-supply, poisoning the inhabitants), ordered so that each is the intention with which the previous one is done, and the last "swallows up" the rest as the intention of the whole.

## Summary
§23 asked whether there are as many actions and intentions as descriptions; §§24–25 raised difficulties. §26 gives the answer (contents line). Take four descriptions: A, moving the arm up and down; B, operating the pump; C, replenishing the water-supply; D, poisoning the inhabitants. These are not four actions but one action under four descriptions, each wider description depending on further circumstances (Speaks; [Wiland & Driver](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md)). The descriptions stand in an *order*: B is the intention with which A is done, C the intention with which B is done, and so on; D, the last term, is the intention of the action described as A or as any intermediate term (contents line). In Holton's report, the final intention "swallows up" the preceding ones. One may correspondingly speak of one intention or of several ([Speaks](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md)).

The ordering relation is not mere conjunction: he moves his arm *because* he is operating the pump, not merely *and* is operating it ([Wiland & Driver](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md)). The "Why?" question traverses the series from A towards D; the corresponding "How?" question (how are you poisoning them? — by replenishing the supply; how that? — by operating the pump) runs it in reverse, from D to A ([Small](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md); [Frey](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md)).

The series has limits at both ends. Below A, descriptions such as contracting particular muscles are not intentional (§23; cf. §30). Beyond D, the chain "breaks": further hopes the agent has (e.g. what he expects to follow from the poisoning) are not further descriptions of *this* action but further intentions or hopes ([Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md); [Millgram](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md)). Where the agent does not know of a consequence (§6), or knows of it but does not care (§25), or the outcome is too uncertain to be stated as "I am Y-ing" (§23), the series stops (Holton's three break points).

Anscombe later says (§42) that this is the same order that Aristotle's practical syllogism sets out; the A–D order is thus the hinge between Part III and the account of practical reasoning (§§33–43).

## Main claim(s)
- One action can have several intentional descriptions forming an ordered series (§26).
- Each term is an intention in the action described by the previous term; the last is the intention of the action under the first or any intermediate description (contents line).
- The "Why?" order (means → end) and the "How?" order (end → means) run in opposite directions over the same series (Small; Frey).

## Examples
- A–D for the pumper: moving arm, operating pump, replenishing supply, poisoning inhabitants.

## Objections and alternatives Anscombe considers
- As many actions as descriptions (§23's question; answered: one action).
- That the poisoning is a separate, later act (§24's timing puzzle).

## In the secondary literature
- [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) — the last term "swallows up" the earlier ones; three natural break points.
- [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) — the A–D order is the object of practical knowledge; "How?" is the reverse of "Why?" (§26); the same order as practical reasoning (§42).
- [Frey 2016](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) — part–whole, means–end unity supplied by practical reason; first in the order of intention is last in the order of execution.
- [Thompson 1998](../../sources/secondary/thompson-1998-rep-anscombe.md) — the "Why?" order is "the reverse of" reasoning from end to means. [Wiland & Driver 2022](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md) — the two "wind up being equivalent". *Editorial note:* these formulations are compatible (same order, opposite direction of travel).
- [Wiland & Driver 2022](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md) — the order "really exists among events", not a sum of physical events plus something in the head; "the order of intention".

- [Ford–Hornsby–Stoutland 2011](../../sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md) — Hornsby on individuation and action as the agent's causing something (§§23–26); Moran & Stone's "nested chain"; Laurence's collective "Why?".

- [Schwenkler c2020 (3:16)](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-c2020-316-interview.md) — doubts that the means–end order elicited by "Why?" marks the intention/foresight line (side-effects).
