# §30 — Not pushing intention back to an initiating movement

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part IV: Knowledge Without Observation](../L1-parts.md#part-iv-knowledge-without-observation) › [G09: §§28–30](../L2-sections/G09-s28-30-knowledge-of-ones-own-action.md) › **§30** · [← §29](§29.md) · [§31 →](§31.md)

*Pages: from p. 53 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: an example to show that it is wrong to push the real intention, or act of will, back to something that initiates the movements.*

## In one sentence
By an example, Anscombe shows that what the agent knows without observation and intends is not some first bodily event (e.g. a muscle contraction) that sets everything else in motion, since agents typically do not know, and need not know, such events at all.

## Summary
§29 rejected locating what is "really" done in an inner act of will. A subtler retreat locates it in the body: what I really do, and know without observation, is the initiating movement — a muscle contraction or the like — while everything further is its observed or inferred effect (contents line). Anscombe calls this pushing the object of knowledge "back and back" ([Setiya 2008](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md), p. 53).

Her counter-example, per [Setiya 2008](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md) (p. 54), involves keeping an object level by means of a pumping contraption: what the agent intends and knows is keeping the thing level, not the movements of his arm by which he achieves it. [Small](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) also refers to a physiologist's experiment in §30 bearing on bodily skill. † The writer recalls the point that one can be asked to contract a particular muscle and be able to do it only by doing something else one knows how to do (e.g. moving a finger), so that the muscle contraction is not what one knows oneself to be doing. The general lesson, in [Speaks](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md)'s words, is that we know what we are doing without knowing *how*, physically, we are doing it (his examples: shooting a basketball, swimming butterfly).

So the object of non-observational knowledge is neither inner (§29) nor a first physical cause (§30); it is the action under its intentional descriptions. This clears the way for §§31–32.

## Main claim(s)
- The real intention is not to be located in an event that initiates the movements (§30, contents line).
- What one does intentionally can be known without observation even when the bodily means are unknown to the agent (Speaks; Setiya).

## Examples
- Keeping an object level with a pumping contraption ([Setiya 2008](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md), p. 54).
- A physiologist's experiment ([Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md), note).

## Objections and alternatives Anscombe considers
- The view that the real act is a physical event (e.g. muscle contraction) that sets things rolling, with the rest known by observation.

## In the secondary literature
- [Setiya 2008](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md) — endorses Anscombe's objection to inference from bodily movement; the "back and back" passage (p. 53) and the levelling contraption (p. 54).
- [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) — third rejected version of the "two-factor" view: physical events that "set things rolling"; we know what we do without knowing how physically.
- [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) — §§29–30: the object of agential knowledge is not bodily movement; §30's physiologist case supports his claim that there must be basic, non-derivatively practicable know-how.
- [Millgram & Bowman 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-bowman-2026-sep-practical-reason-structure-of-actions.md) — below the arm movement the activity is (in their word, attributed to Anscombe) "preintentional": the agent cannot say how he flexes his muscles (unverified wording).
- [Sandis 2015](../../sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md) — the 1958 revision of §19 suggests Anscombe did not count muscle contractions as the outer bodily movement under another description.
- [Ford–Hornsby–Stoutland 2011](../../sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md) — Hornsby's treatment of bodily movement vs. action relates to §§29–30 (via review).
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) — "knowing how I move" as one of the three false avenues (§§29–30).

## Notes and uncertainties
