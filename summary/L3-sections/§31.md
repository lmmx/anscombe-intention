# §31 — The analogy with orders, and why it falls short

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part IV: Knowledge Without Observation](../L1-parts.md#part-iv-knowledge-without-observation) › [G10: §§31–32](../L2-sections/G10-s31-32-orders-and-the-shopping-list.md) › **§31** · [← §30](§30.md) · [§32 →](§32.md)

*Pages: from p. 54 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: an attempted solution that compares the facts which may falsify a statement of intentional action with the facts which may make an order fall to the ground; the solution is inadequate.*

## In one sentence
Anscombe tries out the idea that observed facts like a hole in the pipe relate to "I am replenishing the water-supply" as a false presupposition relates to an order — they make it fail without contradicting it, since the contradictory of an intention is another intention — and then registers that the comparison fails, because an order is not knowledge.

## Summary
"Having raised enough difficulties" ([Millgram](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) cites this as one of the book's section markers), Anscombe tries a solution to the §29 puzzle (contents line). The worry was that observable facts — e.g. that the water is running out of a hole in the pipe ([Setiya 2016](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md), p. 56) — can show the agent's statement "I am replenishing the house water-supply" to be false, so that what he knows without observation must be something less.

The proposal draws on orders (cf. §2). [Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) reports her illustration from *This England*: a soldier court-martialled for disobeying the order "Clench your teeth" — he handed over his false teeth. "My teeth are false" does not contradict the order; it makes it fall to the ground. Likewise "the water is running out of a hole in the pipe" stands to "I am replenishing the water-supply" not as a contradictory. The contradictory of an order is an order ("Do not clench your teeth"), not a report that it cannot be carried out ([Hubbs](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md)); and the contradictory of the agent's description of his intentional action would be "Oh no you aren't", said by someone setting out to make a hole in the pipe with a pick-axe (Holton; Hubbs; [Frey](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md): practical knowledge is contradicted by an opposing intention, not a contrary fact). So observation reports about the pipe would not directly contradict the agent's non-observational statement.

Anscombe then registers the inadequacy (contents line). The parallel fails at the centre, because an order is not a piece of knowledge ([Hubbs](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md)): whereas the agent's "I am replenishing the water-supply" purports to be a true description of what is happening, which the hole in the pipe does show false. The section thus fixes two points that §32 builds on — statements of intentional action resemble orders in how they can fail, yet they are claims to knowledge.

## Main claim(s)
- Facts that defeat a statement of intentional action resemble facts that make an order fall to the ground (§31, contents line).
- The contradictory of a statement of intention is an opposing intention ("Oh no you aren't"), not an observed fact (Holton; Hubbs; Frey).
- The analogy is inadequate because an order is not knowledge (contents line; Hubbs).

## Examples
- The court-martialled soldier with false teeth ordered to clench his teeth (*This England*; [Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md); [Hubbs](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md)).
- The hole in the pipe vs. the person with a pick-axe ([Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md); [Setiya 2016](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md)).

## Objections and alternatives Anscombe considers
- That the hole in the pipe shows the agent knew only his intention or trying (the view the solution is meant to avoid).
- The order-analogy itself, then judged inadequate.

## In the secondary literature
- [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) — presents §31 as "The solution" and reads it as **presupposition failure**: "I am pumping water" presupposes working apparatus; denying a presupposition is not contradicting; the agent knows what he is doing provided the presuppositions hold, without knowing that they hold — a form of Nozick's "open knowledge" (Yablo 2017). Holton notes no one has attributed this to Anscombe, since readers assume she denies practical knowledge is factive. **Divergence from the contents line**, which calls the order-comparison an attempted and inadequate solution; Holton does not mention this.
- [Hubbs 2016](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md) — §31 as a central intention/command parallel; quotes Haddock that contradictories must be "of the same logical order"; the parallel "fails at the centre" because an order is not a piece of knowledge.
- [Millgram 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) — the contradictory of an intention is an intention, not a counter-prediction; following Small 2012, a failed precondition makes only a *means* "fall to the ground".
- [Frey 2016](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) — "contradicted by an opposing intention" as one of six marks of practical knowledge (p. 55).
- [Setiya 2016](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md) — the hole in the pipe (p. 56) can falsify what the pumper says: evidence against interiorising readings.
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) — Ch. 4.3 "Beginning to sketch a solution" (§§31–32): how her treatment of contradicting a description of intentional action addresses the difficulties.
- [Teichmann (ed.) 2022](../../sources/secondary/teichmann-2022-oxford-handbook-anscombe.md) — Roessler on two kinds of error (performance and practical judgement) (§§31–32).
- [Speaks 2009](../../sources/secondary/speaks-2009-guide-1-31.md) — names §31 but does not discuss it.
