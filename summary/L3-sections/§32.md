# §32 — The shopping list and the detective: the idea of practical knowledge

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part IV: Knowledge Without Observation](../L1-parts.md#part-iv-knowledge-without-observation) › [G10: §§31–32](../L2-sections/G10-s31-32-orders-and-the-shopping-list.md) › **§32** · [← §31](§31.md) · [§33 →](§33.md)

*Pages: from p. 56 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: a man goes shopping with a list; how the list relates to what he buys, and how what he buys relates to a list made by a detective who follows him. What kind of discrepancy is there in each case? Is there such a thing as "practical knowledge" in the sense of ancient and medieval philosophy?*

## In one sentence
Two lists with the same content — a shopper's list and a detective's record of the purchases — differ in where the error lies when list and purchases disagree, and Anscombe uses this to introduce the idea of a knowledge whose failures are failures of performance, the "practical knowledge" that modern philosophy has misunderstood.

## Summary
§31's comparison with orders failed because an order is not knowledge. §32 recasts the contrast (contents line). A man goes round a town with a shopping list; a detective follows him and writes down what he buys. At the end the two lists may be identical in content. But they function differently when they disagree with the purchases. If the detective's list does not match what was bought, the mistake is in the list — it is a record, judged by whether it accords with the facts. If the shopper's list does not match, the mistake (if it is one) is not in the list but in what he did: in the performance ([Wiland & Driver](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md), quoting the passage; [Richter](../../sources/secondary/richter-nd-iep-anscombe.md)). † The writer recalls Anscombe's illustration that if the shopper's wife pointed out that the list said butter and he bought margarine, he could not correct matters by altering the list.

Anscombe connects this to failure to carry out an intention: as with failing to execute an accepted order — which [Hubbs](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md) reports she calls precisely analogous, and contrasts with disobedience — the mistake is not one of judgement but of performance ([Setiya SEP](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md), pp. 56–57). She illustrates with saying "Now I press button A" while pressing B: this counts as a mistake only once the wrong button has been pressed ([Setiya 2016](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md), p. 57; [Özaltun](../../sources/secondary/ozaltun-2016-practical-knowledge-section-45.md): "what happens falsifies what is said"). The remark that the mistake lies in the performance is linked by Anscombe to an ancient source (see Notes on attribution).

The section closes by asking whether there is "practical knowledge" in the sense of ancient and medieval philosophy (contents line). Modern philosophy, she says, has "blankly misunderstood" it ([Small](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md)) because it has an "incorrigibly contemplative conception of knowledge" (p. 57; [Hubbs](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md); [Campbell 2024](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2024-practical-knowledge-structural-challenge.md)), on which knowledge must be something judged as such by being in accordance with facts that are prior to it (Small). Practical knowledge is knowledge "in intention" ([Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md), p. 57). §33 states that this can be understood only by first understanding practical reasoning.

## Main claim(s)
- Identical lists can stand in opposite relations to what is done: a record is corrected by the facts; a list that functions as an intention or order makes the discrepancy a fault in the performance (§32).
- When an agent fails to do what he intends (not through false belief about means), the mistake is in the performance, not the judgement (§32, pp. 56–57).
- Modern philosophy's contemplative conception of knowledge prevents it from understanding practical knowledge (§32, p. 57).

## Examples
- The shopper's list and the detective's list.
- "Now I press button A" ([Setiya 2016](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md)).

## Objections and alternatives Anscombe considers
- The contemplative conception of knowledge (all knowledge derived from and checked against prior facts).
- The order-analogy of §31, here refined.

## In the secondary literature
- **"Direction of fit" reading vs. its rejection.**
  - [Wikipedia 2026](../../sources/secondary/wikipedia-2026-anscombe-intention-section.md) calls *Intention* "the classic source" of the belief/desire difference in direction of fit (taken up by Searle), citing §32; low reliability.
  - [Thompson 1998](../../sources/secondary/thompson-1998-rep-anscombe.md) uses "direction of fit, as it is now called" without objection, glossing that the shopper corrects a mismatch by altering the basket (Anscombe herself locates the mistake in the performance and does not prescribe this correction).
  - The SEP *Practical Reason* entry (Wallace & Kiesewetter 2024; one sentence only, reported in the encyclopedia evaluation notes, not saved as a source file) also pairs the idea with Searle.
  - [Wiland & Driver 2022](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md) **reject** the reading: Anscombe "says nothing here" about believing, desiring or mental states; the example shows two ways of *knowing* — knowing by believing what is already true and knowing by intentionally doing — possibly of the same thing (citing Frost 2014). Their own gloss that "one who practically knows doesn't misperform" is stronger than the text.
  - [Campbell 2024](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2024-practical-knowledge-structural-challenge.md) and [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md) warn against attributing a "reverse direction of fit" to practical knowledge; knowledge has no direction of fit and Anscombe does not use the metaphor (citing Alvarez, Boyle & Lavin, Frost).
  - [Millgram 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md): Searle's appropriation goes "against the grain" (citing Vogler 2001); the list concerns fit to what one set oneself to do.
- **Performance vs. judgement.**
  - [Setiya SEP 2022](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md) — Anscombe's own view is a normative contrast: intention sets the standard of success; calls her epistemology here "elusive".
  - [Setiya 2008](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md) — dissents (n. 28): the failed agent makes a mistake of judgement *and* of performance (as does Moran 2004).
  - [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md) — the first formal mark of practical knowledge; Anscombe need not deny that a mistaken judgement also occurs.
  - [Hubbs 2016](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md) — failure to execute an accepted order vs. disobedience; reconciles §32 (recipient's perspective) with §45 (commander's).
  - [Özaltun 2016](../../sources/secondary/ozaltun-2016-practical-knowledge-section-45.md) — in §2, §32 and §45 the performance remark points to another source of mistake, not to knowledge independent of the facts.
  - [Teichmann (ed.) 2022](../../sources/secondary/teichmann-2022-oxford-handbook-anscombe.md) — Roessler adds a second kind of error, of practical judgement; H. Teichman on "Theophrastus's principle".
- **Practical knowledge.** [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) (knowledge "in intention"; contrast with theoretical knowledge judged by accordance with prior facts); [Frey 2016](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) (contemplative-conception passage as the key text); [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum 2023](../../sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md) (practical knowledge as formal cause sets the standard, hence the mistake is in the performance); [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) (shopping list as order vs. record, Ch. 4.3; Pellegrini quotes §32 calling practical knowledge the key); [Schwenkler c2020](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-c2020-316-interview.md) (Aquinas, *ST* I q. 14, as hidden source); [Richter IEP](../../sources/secondary/richter-nd-iep-anscombe.md) (shopping list; "knowledge cannot be falsified by how the facts turn out" — a gloss closer to the §45 puzzle than to §32).

## Notes and uncertainties
- **Attribution of the "performance, not judgement" remark.** [Sandis 2015](../../sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md) refers to it as "Theophrastus' point"; [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) says the passage is in the Aristotelian *Magna Moralia* (1189b) and that Anscombe attributes it to Theophrastus; [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum 2023](../../sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md) say she borrows it from the *Magna Moralia*. These are compatible if Anscombe names Theophrastus for a point found in the *Magna Moralia*; check her wording in §§2, 32, 45.
- [Hubbs 2016](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md) places the first use of the remark in §2; [Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) traces §45's use back to §2's point that the facts, not the words, are impugned.
