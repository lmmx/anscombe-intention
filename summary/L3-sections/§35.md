# §35 — Evaluative first premises, "I want", and reasons that are not practical reasoning

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part V: Practical Reasoning](../L1-parts.md#part-v-practical-reasoning) › [G11: §§33–35](../L2-sections/G11-s33-35-practical-syllogism.md) › **§35** · [← §34](§34.md) · [§36 →](§36.md)

*Pages: from p. 63 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: Aristotle's practical syllogisms have evaluative terms in their first premise. Not every statement of a reason for acting displays practical reasoning. "I want" does not properly belong among the premises, though the first premise must mention something wanted.*

## In one sentence
Aristotle's first premises are evaluative ("suits", "is good for"). The wanting that drives practical reasoning is not itself one of the premises, but what the first premise describes must be wanted if the reasoning is to issue in action. Many genuine reasons for acting involve no such reasoning at all.

## Summary
Anscombe first notes that the first premises of Aristotle's practical syllogisms contain evaluative terms, saying what kind of thing suits or is good for what kind of being (contents line). She glosses these with a "light" sense of "should" (p. 64; Frey) and with the question "with a view to what…?" (p. 63; Frey).

Next, she limits the scope of the notion. Not every statement of a reason for acting shows practical reasoning (contents line). Reasons from Part II are the obvious contrast: backward-looking motives such as revenge or gratitude (§§13–14), and interpretative motives (§§12–14). These are reasons for acting that answer "Why?" (§16) without being calculations of means toward something wanted. This reading is supported by Müller's complaint and Piedrahita's reply, both reported in Ford, Hornsby & Stoutland. Equally, acting "for no particular reason" (§17) is intentional action without practical reasoning, as Schlosser's critics note.

Third, she argues that "I want" does not rightly occur as a premise. The sources consulted do not reproduce her argument for this. [Millgram](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) summarises the point (citing §§37 f.) as: a practical syllogism is not practical unless its major premise marks something wanted, but *that it is wanted* is not part of the syllogism (see Notes). Yet the first premise must still *mention* something wanted. As Elliott puts it, what the starting proposition describes "must be wanted" for the reasoning to lead to any action (p. 66). *Editorial note:* this is the seed of the later claim that wanting relates to "good" as judging relates to "true" (§40). The evaluative term in the premise marks the object *as* desirable; the wanting is what makes the reasoning practical. That claim leads directly into §36's question of what "wanting" is.

## Main claim(s)
- The first premises of Aristotle's practical syllogisms are evaluative (§35, contents line).
- Not all reasons for acting manifest practical reasoning (§35, contents line).
- "I want" is not a premise, but the first premise must mention something wanted (§35, contents line; Elliott p. 66).

## Examples
- Aristotle's evaluative first premises, e.g. dry food suits any man (§33 background).
- A farmer going to market to buy a Jersey cow. [Gosling 1969](../../sources/secondary/gosling-1969-desirability-characterisations.md) uses this case in expounding Anscombe's view and cites pp. 66–75. Philip Clark's NDPR review of [Ford, Hornsby & Stoutland](../../sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md) confirms that the Jersey cow is Anscombe's own example in *Intention* ("I'm buying a Jersey cow" as a forward-looking reason for going to Hereford Market; see Notes). Gosling's page range (pp. 66–75) spans §§35–39, so the example is located only within that stretch, and is listed here because §35 begins on p. 63 and concerns what the first premise mentions. No source fixes its exact section.

## Objections and alternatives Anscombe considers
- Putting "I want X" among the premises (contents line).
- Treating every reason for acting as evidence of a practical syllogism (contents line).

## Cross-references within *Intention*
- §§12–14, 16–17 — backward-looking and interpretative motives; "no particular reason". These are reasons for acting without practical reasoning.
- §§33–34 — the practical syllogism and its starting point.
- §36 — what "wanting" is in the relevant sense.
- §§37–40 — desirability characterisations; wanting:good :: judging:true.

## In the secondary literature
- [Elliott 2016](../../sources/secondary/elliott-2016-anscombe-practical-truth.md) — takes the starting proposition's object "must be wanted" (p. 66) as the core of Anscombe's (expansive) instrumentalism.
- [Frey 2016](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md) — the "light 'should'" (p. 64) and "with a view to what…" (p. 63). The first premise mentions something wanted (§§35–37).
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) — §35 falls under both 5.2 "Calculation" (§§33–35) and 5.3 "The role of 'wanting'" (§§35–36).
- [Müller in Ford, Hornsby & Stoutland 2011](../../sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md) — Anscombe wrongly refuses to count acting on backward-looking reasons as practical reasoning; such responders also "calculate". Piedrahita disagrees.
- [Schlosser 2019](../../sources/secondary/schlosser-2019-sep-agency.md) — equates intentional action with action rationalisable by a sound practical syllogism. This is in tension with the contents line of §35, and the repo's source notes flag it.
- [Setiya 2022](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md) — cites the motivational sense of wanting (§36) that intending entails. Relevant to §35's "something wanted".

## Notes and uncertainties
- The Jersey-cow example is placed by section only approximately (see Examples); the "any farm like mine" wording is Gosling's exposition, not a quotation from Anscombe. An earlier recollection-based explanation of *why* "I want" is not a premise (that it would yield a fact or prediction rather than an action) was not corroborated and has been replaced by Millgram's summary.
- Millgram's summary of the "wanted is not part of the syllogism" argument is in the full manuscript (https://www.elijahmillgram.net/publications/ioi.pdf, n. 21), not in the saved notes; he places the argument at §§37 f., not §35.
- Jersey cow as Anscombe's example: P. Clark, review of *Essays on Anscombe's Intention*, NDPR 2013.02.24, https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/essays-on-anscombe-s-intention/ (on Müller's essay); no § given.
- [Gosling 1969](../../sources/secondary/gosling-1969-desirability-characterisations.md) (open access; *Crítica* 3(9)) cites p. 66 para. 1 for the strong "stopping" claim discussed in §§37–38.
