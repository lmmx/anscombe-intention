# §41 — The mark of practical reasoning: the thing wanted is at a distance

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part V: Practical Reasoning](../L1-parts.md#part-v-practical-reasoning) › [G13: §§41–43](../L2-sections/G13-s41-43-the-order-of-practical-reasoning.md) › **§41** · [← §40](§40.md) · [§42 →](§42.md)

*Pages: from p. 78 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: what marks practical reasoning is that the thing wanted lies at a distance from the particular action done.*

## In one sentence
Reasoning is practical when it bridges a gap between what is wanted and what one can do now. The action is worked out as a way of getting, doing or securing something that lies beyond it. Without such a gap there is no calculation, and so no practical reasoning.

## Summary
Having dealt with wanting and the good (§§36–40), Anscombe returns to the structure of practical reasoning and gives its distinguishing mark. The thing wanted is "at a distance" from the immediate action (p. 79; Elliott; contents line). The reasoning calculates the action as a way of getting, doing or securing what is wanted (Elliott).

Elliott notes three kinds of distance:
- spatial, as with the pump and the house's water supply (§23);
- temporal, as with poisoning the inhabitants for the sake of later good government (§23);
- a "wider description" of the very act, as when lying on the bed counts as resting.

The last kind matters. Practical reasoning need not find a separate means producing a separate effect. It can find that doing this *is*, under a wider description, doing what is wanted.

A consequence that Elliott draws from this stretch: "I ought to do this, so I'll do it" is not practical reasoning, since nothing is calculated and no wider description is reached. This echoes §35's point that not every reason for acting shows practical reasoning (contents line of §35). Campbell (2022) cites §41, with §39, as a place where Anscombe expressly keeps ethics out of the account.

## Main claim(s)
- The mark of practical reasoning is the distance between the thing wanted and the immediate action (§41, contents line; p. 79).
- The distance can be spatial, temporal, or a matter of a wider description of the same act (Elliott).

## Examples
- The pump and water supply; the poisoning and future good government (§23, as used by Elliott for spatial and temporal distance).
- Lying on the bed as resting: a "wider description" (Elliott).
- "I ought to do this, so I'll do it" as a non-case (Elliott; the exact § placement within §§39–41 is not given).

## Objections and alternatives Anscombe considers
- Treating "I ought to do this, so I'll do it" as practical reasoning (Elliott's consequence of this stretch; whether Anscombe herself states it in §41 is not reported).

## In the secondary literature
- [Elliott 2016](../../sources/secondary/elliott-2016-anscombe-practical-truth.md) — the main saved source. He gives the "at a distance from the immediate action" quotation (p. 79), the "getting or doing or securing" formula, the three kinds of distance, and the "ought" non-case. He reads this as "expansive" instrumentalism, including constitutive, wider-description reasoning.
- [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md) — §§39, 41: Anscombe avoids ethics in *Intention*.
- [Wiland & Driver 2022](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md) — implicitly treat §§41–42 as showing that end-to-means deliberation and the "because" order of descriptions are equivalent presentations of one order.
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) — places §41 at the end of 5.4 "The guise of the good" (§§37–41), not with §§42–43. *Editorial note:* this repo's grouping (G13: §§41–43) instead puts §41 with the structural sections, because its contents line concerns the form of reasoning rather than the good.
- [Millgram & Bowman 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-bowman-2026-sep-practical-reason-structure-of-actions.md) — in the pump case, the poisoner's hope for a better future is *not* a further layer of the means–end nesting, because the agent lacks a working sense of how regime change brings it about. This is relevant to where "distance" gives out.
