# §48 — Formally descriptions of executed intentions: "the cause of what it understands"

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part VI: Practical Knowledge](../L1-parts.md#part-vi-practical-knowledge) › [G15: §§46–48](../L2-sections/G15-s46-48-form-of-description-and-practical-knowledge.md) › **§48** · [← §47](§47.md) · [§49 →](§49.md)

*Pages: from p. 87 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: many descriptions of events brought about by human beings are, in their form, descriptions of executed intentions; elucidation of practical knowledge.*

## In one sentence
Where an event's description is of a type to be formally that of an executed intention and the event really is such an execution, the agent's knowledge is, in Aquinas's phrase, "the cause of what it understands" — it is what gives the descriptions under which what happens is the carrying out of an intention — and this practical knowledge is the exercise of a capacity, "knowing one's way about", not a contemplative registering of independent facts.

## Summary
**From form to practical knowledge.** Building on §47, Anscombe observes that a great many descriptions of events effected by human beings are *formally* descriptions of executed intentions (contents line; p. 87). This lets her state the positive account of practical knowledge promised since §32. Where (a) the description of an event is of a type to be formally the description of an executed intention, and (b) the event is actually the execution of an intention, then Aquinas's account of practical knowledge holds: practical knowledge is "the cause of what it understands", unlike "speculative" knowledge, which is derived from the objects known (§48, p. 87; conditions (a)/(b) as set out by [Setiya 2016](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md); the Aquinas sentence as quoted by [Small](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) and [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum](../../sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md)).

**What the causality amounts to.** Anscombe insists this means more than that practical knowledge is observed to be a necessary condition of the production of various results (§48, per Small). Rather, it is the agent's knowledge of what he is doing that gives the descriptions under which what is going on is the execution of an intention; without it, what happens does not come under the description "execution of intentions" (pp. 87–88, per [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md) and [Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md)).

**Failure as the exception.** Returning to §45's problem, she says that failure to execute intentions is "necessarily the rare exception" — though failure to attain what is aimed at under its desirability characterisation (health, pleasure and the like) is common. What is rare is for one's performance, under its more immediate descriptions, not to be what one supposes (p. 87, per Holton and Setiya). The §45 builder case, reliant on a superhuman imagination, is acknowledged to be "a very improbable one" (per [Özaltun](../../sources/secondary/ozaltun-2016-practical-knowledge-section-45.md)).

**The "extra feature" temptation.** The rival she has in view treats being intentional as a mere extra feature of events whose description would otherwise be the same — a feature one would then try to analyse, e.g. as causation by an inner state (p. 88, per Setiya; cf. §§19, 47).

**Practical knowledge and know-how.** A man has practical knowledge who knows how to do things; but this is an insufficient description, since one could lecture on how to do something and yet be helpless to do it. Practical knowledge is a capacity whose exercise is nothing but the doing or supervising of the operations of which one has it — operations which are formally subject to "Why?" and display the A–D order, unlike mere effects such as reciting the alphabet (p. 88, per Small and Setiya). And although "practical knowledge" is usually spoken of in connection with specialised skills, the notion is not confined to them: intentional action always presupposes "knowing one's way about" the matters described, and this knowledge is exercised in the action (p. 89, per Small).

## Main claim(s)
- Many descriptions of human doings are formally descriptions of executed intentions (§48).
- Under conditions (a) and (b), practical knowledge is "the cause of what it understands" (Aquinas), unlike speculative knowledge derived from its objects (§48).
- This causality is more than being a necessary condition: the knowledge supplies the descriptions under which the event is the execution of an intention (§48).
- Failure to execute intentions is necessarily the rare exception (§48).
- Practical knowledge is an exercised capacity related to, but not exhausted by, knowing how; it is presupposed by all intentional action, not only skilled performance (§48).

## Examples
- Writing one's name on the board (knowledge of a completed act; cited for §48 by [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum](../../sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md): "the knowledge that I wrote my name on the board (§48)"; live entry linked in Notes).
- Lecturing on how to do something while being helpless to do it (§48, per Small).
- Reciting the alphabet as a mere effect, contrasted with operations subject to "Why?" (§48, per Small).
- Health, pleasure as goals whose attainment commonly fails, unlike execution of intentions (§48, per Holton).
- The §45 builder, now called "very improbable" (§48).

## Objections and alternatives Anscombe considers
- Intentionality as an "extra feature" of events otherwise identically described (§48).
- Practical knowledge as merely an observed necessary condition of results (§48).
- Practical knowledge as specialist know-how only (§48).
- The §45 worry that failure makes the knowledge independent of what happens (addressed by "rare exception").

## In the secondary literature
**Formal vs efficient cause; always vs sometimes.**
- [Setiya 2016](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2016-anscombe-practical-knowledge.md) — the causality is **formal**, and **conditional**: it holds only where (a) and (b) are met, else (a) would be redundant. In the basic case (descriptions answering "Why?") it always holds; knowledge of e.g. sliding on ice rests on it but is not causal. Names Moran 2004, Stoutland 2011, McDowell 2011 and Rödl 2011 as reading it as unrestricted. Glosses "rare exception" as safety, needing neither reliabilism nor efficient causation (contra Velleman 2007); links p. 88 to knowing how.
- [Setiya 2022 SEP](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md) and [Setiya 2008/2009](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md) — formal not efficient (pp. 87–8), citing Anscombe's "The Causation of Action" (1983); lists a dozen conflicting interpretations.
- [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md) — §48 is the *culmination*, replacing the negative "without observation" with a positive characterisation; with Moran, **formal cause**: the agent's knowing is constitutive of the action's being intentional, hence non-observational *because* practical. Resolves the §28/§48 tension in favour of §48 (intentional action presupposes know-how).
- [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md) — "cause of what it understands" is the second formal mark of practical knowledge (with "mistake in performance", §32); formal, internal causation; explained by knowledge "in intention".
- [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum 2023](../../sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md) — quote §48 at length; Anscombe "appears to define" intentional action as manifesting practical knowledge; formal cause; whether also efficient is disputed (Setiya sceptical; Piñeros Glasscock 2020 in favour).
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) — argues Anscombe meant practical knowledge as **both formal and efficient** cause, yet concludes knowing one's act is not strictly required for acting intentionally (abstract only); in the [3:16 interview](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-c2020-316-interview.md) he still denies agents always know what they intentionally do and partly retracts the guide's "practical *and* perceptual" view.
- [Frey 2016](../../sources/secondary/frey-2016-action-practical-knowledge-good.md), [Hubbs 2016](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md) — endorse the Thomistic formula as vindicated; Frey: action "comes to be only in the agent's knowledge that it is coming to be"; agent as a cause not understood solely as efficient.
- [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) — uses §48's condition (b) and "rare exception" to complete his reading of §45: where there is no bad luck, practical knowledge is categorical.
- [Millgram 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) — does not treat §48 directly; applies "formal cause" to intention itself (control structure of action).

**Aquinas source.**
- [Small 2020](../../sources/secondary/small-2020-action-practical-knowledge.md), [Hubbs 2016](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md), [Elliott 2016](../../sources/secondary/elliott-2016-anscombe-practical-truth.md) — the quoted formula is from *ST* I-II q. 3 a. 5 obj. 1.
- [Schwenkler c.2020](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-c2020-316-interview.md) — the background doctrine is Aquinas on divine cognition, *ST* I q. 14. (*Editorial note:* the two claims are compatible — one locates the quoted phrase, the other the underlying theory.) [Teichmann 2022](../../sources/secondary/teichmann-2022-oxford-handbook-anscombe.md) lists Haldane's chapter on Anscombe and Aquinas as background.

**Know-how.** [Setiya 2008](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2008-practical-knowledge.md) — Anscombe gestures at the connection (pp. 88–89) "without much development". Small — favours a "bifurcationist" view of know-how. **Mis-citation flag:** [Campbell 2024](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2024-practical-knowledge-structural-challenge.md) places "the exercise of knowledge how" at §45.

**Popular gloss.** [Wikipedia](../../sources/secondary/wikipedia-2026-anscombe-intention-section.md) connects §48 to a Searlean "direction of fit" reading of §32 — a reading rejected by [Wiland & Driver](../../sources/secondary/wiland-driver-2022-sep-anscombe.md) and [Campbell 2024](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2024-practical-knowledge-structural-challenge.md).
