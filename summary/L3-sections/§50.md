# §50 — Return to intention for the future: a prediction to which "Why?" applies

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part VII: Voluntary Action and Intention for the Future](../L1-parts.md#part-vii-voluntary-action-and-intention-for-the-future) › [G17: §§50–52](../L2-sections/G17-s50-52-intention-for-the-future-revisited.md) › **§50** · [← §49](§49.md) · [§51 →](§51.md)

*Pages: from p. 90 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: back to the expression of intention for the future; what has been said of intention in present action carries over to intention for the future; a prediction counts as an expression of intention when the question "Why?" applies to it.*

## In one sentence
Declaring the inquiry into intentional action and intention-with-which complete, Anscombe returns to the first of her three heads and argues that it needs no separate theory: an expression of intention is a prediction of one's own action to which the special "Why?" applies, and intention for the future is the same concept as intention in present action.

## Summary
The book opened (§§2–4) with expressions of intention for the future — statements such as "I am going to…", classed in §2 as a species of "prediction" alongside estimates and orders, and distinguished from estimates by the kind of justification, if any, given for them (§3). Anscombe then turned to intentional action because the expression of intention gave no direct route into the concept (§§3–4). §50 marks the return (contents line); she signals that the investigation of intentional action and of the intention with which one acts has now been completed (per [Holton](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md); cf. [Millgram](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) on the "now I will return…" marker).

The main thesis is that what has been said about intention in present action applies to intention for the future (contents line). The criterion is the one used throughout: a statement about one's own future action is an expression of intention, and not a mere estimate of what will happen, when the question "Why?" in the special sense applies to it — when it is apt to ask "Why are you going to do that?" and be given a reason, or "no particular reason" (contents line; [Thompson 1998](../../sources/secondary/thompson-1998-rep-anscombe.md)). So the distinction drawn intuitively in §2, which Anscombe refused to take as foundational, is now grounded in the account of intentional action: future intention is the further end of the A–D series pressed beyond what is now happening (cf. §22; §23 on the not-quite-sharp line between "I am doing" and "I am going to do").

Early in the book Anscombe had allowed that a man can form an intention which he does nothing to carry out, the intention being complete although it remains "a purely interior thing" (p. 9). §50 says only briefly that what has been said about intention in acting "applies also to intention in a proposed action" (p. 90) — both quoted by Moran & Stone (see Notes), who note that she offers almost no separate account of such intention.

## Main claim(s)
- The inquiry into intentional action and intention-with-which is complete (§50, per Holton).
- Intention for the future is not a further, distinct phenomenon; the account of present intention applies to it (§50).
- A prediction is an expression of intention when "Why?" (special sense) applies to it (§50).

## Examples
- Ordinary first-person future statements ("I am going to…") as candidates for the applicability of "Why?" (contents line). No source consulted reports specific examples from §50.

## Objections and alternatives Anscombe considers
- The intuitive prediction/expression-of-intention distinction of §2, now replaced by the "Why?" criterion.

## In the secondary literature
**Unity of the three heads.**
- [Setiya 2022 SEP](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md) — Anscombe does not treat future intention as needing further explanation once intentional action and intention-with-which are understood (pp. 90–4); she "appears to solve the problem of unity without acknowledging intention as a mental state". Setiya then criticises the neo-Anscombean development (Thompson; Moran & Stone; Falvey) on which intending is already being embarked on doing.
- [Thompson 1998](../../sources/secondary/thompson-1998-rep-anscombe.md) — if "Why?" applies to a first-person future-tense description, it is an expression of intention, not a mere prediction; future intention is derivative of intentional action.
- [Holton 2025](../../sources/secondary/holton-2025-cambridge-lectures.md) — maps expression of intention to §§2–4 and §§50–52; the intention-with-which discussion runs explicitly to §50 (against Wiseman's §§22–40).
- [Millgram 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) — §§2 and 50–52 form the opening and closing frame of the book; the "now I will return" marker shows the book executing its own A–D order; speculatively, the discussion of prediction shows determinism and the "logical freedom" of action "pass each other by".
- [Ford, Hornsby & Stoutland 2011](../../sources/secondary/ford-hornsby-stoutland-2011-essays.md) — Moran & Stone's exegesis covers §§2–4, 22, 50–52 (reviewers' description; Clark presses the never-acted-on intention as a problem case).
- [Schwenkler 2019](../../sources/secondary/schwenkler-2019-guide.md) — Ch. 7.2 "Intention for the future" (§§50–52): intention is expressed in a statement about what will happen ("factualism") (abstract and review).
- [Hubbs 2016](../../sources/secondary/hubbs-2016-intentions-and-commands.md) — sees the command–intention comparisons framing the move back to future intention (§§45–50); formed intentions for later are commitments to act when circumstances are right.
- [Campbell 2022](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2022-practical-knowledge-practical-truth.md) and [Campbell 2024](../../sources/secondary/campbell-2024-practical-knowledge-structural-challenge.md) — the same capacity yields knowledge of what one will do; prospective practical knowledge is also knowledge "in intention".
