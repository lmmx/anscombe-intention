# §51 — "I just want to, that's all": reasonless future intention

[Overview](../L0-overview.md) › [Part VII: Voluntary Action and Intention for the Future](../L1-parts.md#part-vii-voluntary-action-and-intention-for-the-future) › [G17: §§50–52](../L2-sections/G17-s50-52-intention-for-the-future-revisited.md) › **§51** · [← §50](§50.md) · [§52 →](§52.md)

*Pages: from p. 90 (1963 2nd ed. / Harvard 2000 pagination). Anscombe's contents line, paraphrased: the answer "I just want to, that's all" given about an expression of intention for the future.*

## In one sentence
Anscombe examines the reply "I just want to, that's all" to "Why are you going to do that?", the future-tense counterpart of acting "for no particular reason", and asks whether and when it leaves an expression of intention intelligible.

## Summary
§50 made "Why?"-applicability the mark of an expression of intention for the future. An immediate test case is the answer that gives no further reason: asked why I am going to do something, I say "I just want to, that's all" (contents line). This parallels the present-tense case of acting "for no particular reason" (§17), where "Why?" is held to apply even though the answer is negative, as opposed to being refused (contrast "I didn't know I was doing that", §6).

According to [Millgram](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md), §51 suggests that desirability characterisations are in order even for actions about which one might say "Oh, I just wanted to": Anscombe allows the further question "Well, at least what's pleasant or interesting about it?" (§51, quoted in Millgram's full text; see Notes), in the manner of the desirability characterisations of §§37–40. †Whether she says in so many words that such an answer grants "Why?" application, so that the statement remains an expression of intention rather than an estimate, is not reported by any source consulted.

## Main claim(s)
- "I just want to" is a possible answer to "Why?" about a future intention (§51).
- It still invites questions of desirability, e.g. what is pleasant or interesting about it (§51, per Millgram).

## Examples
- The reply "I just want to, that's all" (§51, contents line).

## Objections and alternatives Anscombe considers
- None reported by the sources consulted.

## In the secondary literature
- [Millgram 2026](../../sources/secondary/millgram-2026-intention-of-intention.md) — "I just want to" (§51) still takes, or invites, a desirability characterisation; on his reading knowing what one is doing is the desirability characterisation of intentional action as such.
- [Piñeros Glasscock & Tenenbaum 2023](../../sources/secondary/pineros-glasscock-tenenbaum-2023-sep-action.md) — cite §§51–52 for Anscombe's apparent inclusion of knowledge of future actions, against restricting practical knowledge to actions in progress.
- [Setiya 2022 SEP](../../sources/secondary/setiya-2022-sep-intention.md) — treats §§50–52 together (pp. 90–4) as showing future intention needs no separate account; also notes the problem idle action "for no particular reason" (§17) poses for reducing intentional action to intention-with-which.
- Otherwise, the saved sources do not discuss §51 specifically.

## Notes and uncertainties
- † = writer's recollection of the text, not corroborated by the contents line or a saved source; check against the book. One † remains: that "I just want to" grants "Why?" application, keeping the statement an expression of intention. It is kept because it follows the §17 treatment of "no particular reason" and the §50 criterion, but it is not verified. An earlier objection (a reasonless future statement must be a prediction) was not corroborated and has been removed, as was a gloss that the answer is intelligible only against what can be wanted.
- Millgram's quotation of §51 ("Well, at least what's pleasant or interesting about it?") is in his full manuscript, n. 20: https://www.elijahmillgram.net/publications/ioi.pdf. The saved notes record only that §51 "invites desirability questions".
- No edition variants recorded by [Sandis 2015](../../sources/secondary/sandis-2015-klesis-anscombes-intentions.md).
